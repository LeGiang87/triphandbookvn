Hanoi — where to stay

Stay in or near the Old Quarter if it is your first time: you can walk to the lake, the food and the sights, and Grab covers the rest. The French Quarter (around the Opera House and Trang Tien) is quieter and more polished, with big hotels. Tay Ho (West Lake) suits longer stays and people who want space and cafes, but you will take a Grab everywhere.

Budget travelers will find dorm beds in the Old Quarter for roughly 150,000-300,000 VND (~$6-12) per night and private rooms in small hotels for 400,000-800,000 VND (~$15-31). Mid-range boutique hotels in the Old Quarter run about 1,000,000-2,500,000 VND (~$38-96) per night. High-end hotels run from about 3,500,000 VND (~$135) per night and up. Prices move a lot with weekends, holidays and Tet — check again when you book. Compare stays on the official homepages of Agoda and Booking.com (plain links, no affiliate code).

Ha Long — where to stay

Stay in Bai Chay if you are here for the cruise: the ports, the beach, Sun World and the restaurants are all there. Stay on Reu Island if you want a resort escape (Vinpearl runs a speedboat from Bai Chay Wharf). Stay in Hon Gai if you want the local city and lower prices, and do not mind a taxi to the ports.

Budget travelers will find hostels and small hotels in Bai Chay for roughly 250,000-500,000 VND (~$10-19) per night. Mid-range hotels run about 800,000-2,000,000 VND (~$31-77). High-end resorts run from about 2,400,000 VND (~$92) per night and up — Vinpearl Resort & Spa Ha Long lists rooms from about 3,200,000 VND (~$123) per night. Prices move a lot with weekends, holidays and the cruise season — check again when you book. Compare stays on the official homepages of Agoda and Booking.com (plain links, no affiliate code).

Ninh Binh — where to stay

Choose the area first, then the room. Tam Coc — around the boat pier and Ngo Dong Road — suits first-timers, backpackers and couples: walk to the boat pier, plenty of places to eat, rent a bike right where you stay. Homestays and family rooms run 200,000-500,000 VND (~$8-19) per night; mid-range hotels and resorts, for example bungalows in the fields like Tam Coc Garden, run 500,000-1,500,000 VND (~$19-58); high-end, for example Ninh Binh Hidden Charm, starts from 1,500,000 VND (~$58).

The Trang An area — the valley around Trang An Boat Pier — suits people who want quiet and nature, staying between the mountains. There are fewer places to eat around — meals are mostly at your stay, or a ride away. Homestays and bungalows typically run 300,000-800,000 VND (~$12-31).

The town center (Hoa Lu Ward) suits travelers catching an early or late train who want city comforts and soft prices. It has the least scenery, and you will travel 7-10 km to the sights. Ordinary hotels run 300,000-700,000 VND (~$12-27).

Around Hang Mua suits photographers: stay at the foot of the mountain and climb at sunrise before the tour groups arrive. Homestays with field views run 250,000-650,000 VND (~$10-25).

Booking tips: pick a stay that lends or rents bicycles and motorbikes on site — it saves real time and money on getting around. In rice season (May-June) and festival season (January-March lunar calendar), field-view rooms in Tam Coc sell out early on weekends, so book 2-3 weeks ahead. Prices above are reference ranges from 2026 accommodation listings and move a lot with weekends and seasons — check again on the booking site when you travel. You can compare stays on the official homepages of Agoda and Booking.com (plain links, no affiliate code).

Sa Pa — where to stay

Stay in the town centre if it is your first visit — everything is walkable, and buses and treks pick up from your hotel. Stay in Ta Van or Lao Chai if you want to wake up in the valley; homestays there are simpler but the setting is unbeatable. Book ahead for September–November weekends.

Nha Trang — where to stay

Stay on or just behind Tran Phu Street for the beach, the promenade, and the restaurants. The northern end (near the Sheraton and InterContinental) is quieter and more upmarket; the middle stretch around Biet Thu and Hung Vuong is the backpacker and mid-range zone, with hostels, bars, and cheap eats; the southern end near Cau Da Port is closest to the boat pier and VinWonders cable car. Ho Chi Minh City — where to stay

Stay in or near District 1 for a first visit — everything is walkable, and Grab rides anywhere else in the center cost very little. The Bui Vien / Pham Ngu Lao area is the backpacker hub: dorm beds run roughly 150,000–300,000 VND (~$6–$12) a night and budget private rooms 400,000–700,000 VND (~$15–$27). Around Dong Khoi and Nguyen Hue you pay more for quieter streets and river views. Thao Dien, across the river, is the expat neighborhood — leafier, calmer, and a metro ride from the center.

Phu Quoc — where to stay

Where you stay on Phu Quoc decides your holiday. Duong Dong / Long Beach is the best base for a first visit: restaurants, the night market, and the airport are all close, and you can reach anywhere on the island from here. An Thoi / Sunset Town in the south suits the cable car, Sao Beach, and resort stays. Ganh Dau / Bai Dai in the north suits families doing Vinpearl Safari and VinWonders, with big resorts and Grand World next door. Ong Lang is the quiet middle — boutique resorts on a pretty beach, good for couples.

Guesthouses around Duong Dong run roughly 400,000–800,000 VND (~$15–$31) a night; mid-range resorts 1,200,000–2,500,000 VND (~$46–$96); luxury resorts from 4,000,000 VND (~$154) and up. Book through Agoda (https://www.agoda.com) or Booking.com (https://www.booking.com) — both list the island's full range, and prices swing a lot by season.
