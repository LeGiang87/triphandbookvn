Da Nang or Hoi An: Where Should You Base Yourself in Central Vietnam?

You fly into Da Nang either way — Hoi An has no airport — so this is the first real decision of a central Vietnam trip: sleep in the city, or sleep in the old town? They are only 30 km apart, a 30–45 minute drive, and since 1 July 2026 Hoi An is officially part of Da Nang City. But they feel like different countries, and where you sleep decides what your days look like.

The short answer: base in Da Nang if you want the beach, the nightlife and easy day trips — especially with kids, or if you are flying in and out. Base in Hoi An if the old town is the point of the trip — couples, food lovers and anyone who wants to slow down — and stay at least one night so you see it lit up. With 4 nights or more, do both: split the stay instead of choosing.

Da Nang vs Hoi An: side-by-side comparison

Da Nang Hoi An
The feel A modern beach city: wide roads, high-rises, beachfront hotels, a riverfront that lights up at night. A 400-year-old trading town: yellow walls, wooden shophouses, silk lanterns, no cars in the old streets at pedestrian hours.
Getting in The airport (DAD) is 3 km away, 10–15 minutes, Grab 50,000–90,000 VND (~$2–3.50) to the centre. The train station is in town too. No airport, no train station. Fly/train to Da Nang, then Grab or taxi 30 km, 30–45 minutes, 300,000–400,000 VND (~$12–15).
The beach My Khe Beach is in the city — long, sandy, lifeguards on duty in the day, free. Best swimming early morning or late afternoon. An Bang Beach is 4 km from the Old Town (Grab 50,000–80,000 VND, ~$2–3). Quieter, with beach bars at sunset. Cua Dai is closer but crowded.
The big sights Ba Na Hills and the Golden Bridge (cable car ~950,000–1,000,000 VND, ~$37–38), Marble Mountains (40,000 VND, ~$1.50), Linh Ung Pagoda and the 67 m Lady Buddha (free), Dragon Bridge fire show at 21:00 on weekend evenings. The Ancient Town itself (walking the streets is free; each heritage site 20,000 VND, ~$0.80, since 10 Aug 2026 — the old 120,000 VND ticket is gone), My Son Sanctuary (150,000 VND, ~$5.80, 45–60 min away), basket boats at Bay Mau Coconut Forest (~150,000–200,000 VND per boat for two).
Evenings Seafood dinners on the beach road, the Dragon Bridge show, bars and a proper city nightlife. Lantern-lit streets, the Hoai River boat ride (~170,000 VND for up to 3 people), the night market, tailors and coffee houses. Cars are banned from the main old streets 09:00–11:00 and 15:00–21:30.
Food Mi quang ~40,000 VND (~$1.50) a bowl, bun cha ca ~35,000 VND (~$1.35), seafood priced by weight — agree the price first. Cao lau, white rose dumplings and the famous banh mi (30,000–50,000 VND, ~$1.15–1.90). Morning Glory does cao lau and white rose in one sitting, ~90,000–300,000 VND a dish.
A night's sleep Dorm ~$9–15; budget private room ~$22–40; mid-range 3–4 star ~$40–75. More choice, more competition, newer rooms for the money. Dorm ~$8–14; budget private ~$20–35; mid-range ~$35–70 — but rooms inside the Ancient Town carry a 20–30% heritage premium. One or two blocks outside the old town, the same quality costs 20–30% less.
Day trips from base Hue by train over the Hai Van Pass (2.5–3 hours, 100,000–250,000 VND, ~$4–10), Ba Na Hills, Marble Mountains, Son Tra Peninsula — all inside an hour. My Son, Tra Que Vegetable Village, the coconut forest, An Bang Beach — all within an hour. Da Nang itself is a 30–45 minute trip the other way.
Getting around Flat and compact, Grab everywhere, a ride across the centre 50,000–100,000 VND (~$2–4). Motorbike rental ~150,000–200,000 VND (~$6–8) a day. On foot in the Old Town, bicycle to the beaches and villages. Grab works for cars; a Grab to An Bang is ~50,000–80,000 VND.
Best for Families, beach lovers, first-timers who want Vietnam without the chaos, anyone using the city as a launchpad. Couples, culture and food lovers, photographers, anyone who wants to slow down after the big cities.
Watch out for October is the wettest month (~628 mm of rain); November ~448 mm. Typhoon season September–December — check the forecast. October–November floods: the Old Town sits barely a metre above the river, and the lowest streets flood most years. Floods usually recede in 1–3 days, but tailors and riverside shops close while the water is up.

Da Nang or Hoi An: choose in 3 questions

Answer these three and the base picks itself:

1. What is the trip actually about — the beach or the old town?

If your best days are swimming, sunset seafood and a city that stays up late, that is Da Nang. If your best evenings are lantern light, a slow walk and a tailor fitting, that is Hoi An — and it only happens if you sleep there, because the town changes completely after the day-trippers leave.

2. Who is travelling with you?

Kids, a big group, or anyone who needs easy transport and a proper beach at the door: Da Nang. The airport is 10 minutes away, the beach is in the city, and everything is a short Grab ride. A couple on a slow trip, or food lovers who plan meals like sightseeing: Hoi An.

3. How many nights do you have in the centre?

Two nights or fewer: pick one base and day-trip the other — the transfer is only 30–45 minutes, so you lose nothing by choosing. Three nights: still one base, with a full day in the other town. Four or more: split the stay (below) instead of choosing at all.

The split stay: Da Nang and Hoi An in 4+ nights

This is what most experienced travellers actually do, and it costs less than it looks. Three nights in Hoi An, two in Da Nang is the classic shape — or the reverse if the beach matters more to you. Each move costs about half a day (pack, check out, 30–45 minute drive, check in), so never split a trip shorter than 4 nights, and never move more than once.

A workable 5-night shape: nights 1–3 in Hoi An (Old Town, My Son, a tailor fitting — tailoring runs on a 2–3 day cycle of fitting, adjustment and collection, so start it on day one), nights 4–5 in Da Nang (Ba Na Hills, Marble Mountains, My Khe Beach, and the airport 10 minutes away on your last morning). If your flight leaves early, end in Da Nang — an early Hoi An departure means a 04:30 taxi and a prayer about traffic.

Da Nang vs Hoi An by season: when the weather decides

February to August is dry in both places, and the choice is pure taste: beach city or old town. From September to December, base on higher ground and keep plans loose. Rain that floods Hoi An's lanes can also slow the road between the two towns, and a single storm can cost you a day. In October and November specifically: book refundable rooms, ask for an upper floor in a riverside guesthouse in Hoi An, and keep a buffer day in the middle of the trip, not the end. If your dates are fixed in October–November and the Old Town is the reason you are going, consider moving them — the cheapest hotel rates of the year coincide exactly with the worst weather. Getting to Da Nang and Hoi An

Fly into Da Nang International Airport, 3 km from the city centre — Grab to downtown is 50,000–90,000 VND (~$2–3.50). Hoi An has no airport or train station: you arrive via Da Nang, then a 30–45 minute Grab (300,000–400,000 VND, ~$12–15). The train from Hanoi takes about 16 hours; from Hue it is 2.5–3 hours over the Hai Van Pass (100,000–250,000 VND) — sit on the sea side, left going south.

Da Nang International Airport

  • Address: Hoa Thuan Tay Ward, Hai Chau District, Da Nang
  • Google Maps: Open in Google Maps
  • Price: Grab to the city centre 50,000–90,000 VND (~$2–3.50); metered taxi 60,000–150,000 VND (~$2.30–5.80), plus a small airport exit fee of 10,000–15,000 VND (~$0.40–0.60)
  • Hours: Open 24 hours
  • Type: station
  • Tip: Ride-hailing pick-up is in a designated zone about 50 m from the arrivals hall — follow the signs instead of taking the first taxi that approaches you inside the terminal.

Da Nang Railway Station

  • Address: 791 Hai Phong Street, Thanh Khe District, Da Nang (main gate at 200 Hai Phong Street)
  • Google Maps: Open in Google Maps
  • Price: Da Nang–Hue train 100,000–250,000 VND (~$4–10)
  • Hours: Open daily from early morning to late evening
  • Type: station
  • Tip: Book the daytime SE19/SE20 services for the Hai Van Pass views; night trains pass the coast in the dark.

Hoi An Ancient Town

  • Address: Tran Phu Street, Hoi An Ancient Town, Da Nang
  • Google Maps: Open in Google Maps
  • Price: Walking the streets is free. Since 10 August 2026, entry to each heritage building costs 20,000 VND (~$0.80) per person, the same for Vietnamese and foreign visitors; children under 16 free. The old 120,000 VND ticket is no longer sold.
  • Hours: Ticket booths 07:00–21:30 in summer, to 21:00 in winter; heritage sites about 08:30–17:30
  • Type: attraction
  • Tip: The 20,000 VND ticket covers one of 22 heritage sites — the assembly halls, old houses and museums. Buy tickets only at the official booths. On the full-moon lantern night each month, the electric lights go off around 18:00–22:00 and the town glows by lantern alone; remaining 2026 dates are 22 November and 22 December.

Day trips & sights in this guide

Everything below is a day trip from one base or the other. Addresses and maps so you can check the distance yourself before you choose where to sleep:

Ba Na Hills & the Golden Bridge

  • Address: Ba Na Hills, Hoa Ninh Commune, Hoa Vang District, Da Nang (about 25 km west of the city centre)
  • Google Maps: Open in Google Maps
  • Price: Cable-car ticket about 950,000–1,000,000 VND (~$37–38) per adult; children 1–1.4 m about 750,000–800,000 VND (~$29–31); under 1 m free. Da Nang residents pay less. Combo ticket with lunch buffet about 1,250,000–1,300,000 VND (~$48–50). Prices vary by source — check at the ticket counter.
  • Hours: Cable car 08:00–22:00 daily; Golden Bridge area 08:00–18:30
  • Type: attraction
  • Tip: Go early — the bridge is quietest before 09:00, and the mountain is often clearest in the morning before the clouds roll in. Since 2026, tickets are valid for 3 consecutive days with unlimited cable-car rides.

Marble Mountains (Ngu Hanh Son)

  • Address: 81 Huyen Tran Cong Chua Street, Ngu Hanh Son District, Da Nang (about 8 km south of the centre, on the road to Hoi An)
  • Google Maps: Open in Google Maps
  • Price: Thuy Son Mountain entry 40,000 VND (~$1.50) per adult; students 10,000 VND (~$0.40); under 6 free. Elevator 15,000 VND (~$0.60) one way, 30,000 VND (~$1.15) return. Am Phu Cave is a separate 20,000 VND (~$0.80).
  • Hours: 07:00–17:30 daily (some sources say 17:00; ticket sales stop around 17:00)
  • Type: attraction
  • Tip: Climb to Huyen Khong Cave between 09:00 and 11:00, when sunlight pours through the cave opening. Bring cash — the gate is cash-only. Wear shoes with grip; the stone steps are steep and slippery when wet.

Dragon Bridge

  • Address: Nguyen Van Linh Street, Hai Chau District, Da Nang (spans the Han River)
  • Google Maps: Open in Google Maps
  • Price: Free
  • Hours: Open 24 hours; fire and water show on weekend evenings at 21:00 (Friday–Sunday according to most sources; some list Saturday–Sunday only — check locally)
  • Type: attraction
  • Tip: Watch from the riverside walkway on the east bank for the full view of the dragon. Arrive by 20:30 for a good spot, and expect crowds.

My Khe Beach

  • Address: Vo Nguyen Giap Street, Son Tra District, Da Nang
  • Google Maps: Open in Google Maps
  • Price: Free
  • Hours: Open 24 hours; lifeguards on duty during the day
  • Type: attraction
  • Tip: The best swimming is early morning or late afternoon, when the sun is off the sand. The beach is long, so walk south past the hotels for quieter stretches. The sea is roughest from October to December.

My Son Sanctuary

  • Address: Duy Phu Commune, Duy Xuyen District, Da Nang (about 35–40 km southwest of Hoi An, 45–60 minutes by car)
  • Google Maps: Open in Google Maps
  • Price: 150,000 VND (~$5.80) foreign adult; 100,000 VND (~$3.85) Vietnamese adult; children 5–15: 30,000 VND (~$1.15); under 5 free. Includes the electric shuttle, museum and Cham dance shows at 09:30 and 10:30.
  • Hours: 06:00–18:00 daily, last tickets 17:00 (some sources list closing at 17:00)
  • Type: attraction
  • Tip: Go before 08:15 or after 15:30 to avoid the tour-bus crowds and the midday heat. The brick Hindu temples of the Champa kingdom, built between the 4th and 13th centuries, sit in a jungle valley — allow at least 2 hours.

An Bang Beach

  • Address: Nguyen Phan Vinh Street, Cam An Ward, Hoi An, Da Nang (about 4 km from the Old Town)
  • Google Maps: Open in Google Maps
  • Price: Free
  • Hours: Open 24 hours
  • Type: attraction
  • Tip: Hoi An's best beach — long, sandy and much quieter than the town. Come for sunset, when the fishing boats come in and the beach bars light up. The sea is calmest from February to August.

Bay Mau Coconut Forest (Cam Thanh)

  • Address: DX18 Road, Cam Thanh Commune, Hoi An, Da Nang (about 5 km east of the Old Town)
  • Google Maps: Open in Google Maps
  • Price: Entry about 30,000 VND (~$1.15) per person; basket boat about 150,000–200,000 VND (~$6–8) per boat for two people, 45–60 minutes. Prices vary by operator — confirm before boarding. A tip of 20,000–50,000 VND (~$0.80–1.90) for the rower is customary.
  • Hours: Not verified
  • Type: attraction
  • Tip: The round basket boats (thung chai) spin, the rowers sing, and you will get splashed — wear clothes you do not mind getting wet. Book through your hotel or a reputable operator rather than the touts at the car park.

Tra Que Vegetable Village

  • Address: Hai Ba Trung Road, Cam Ha Commune, Hoi An, Da Nang (about 2.5 km from the Ancient Town)
  • Google Maps: Open in Google Maps
  • Price: Not verified — cooking classes and farming experiences are sold by local operators; agree the price before booking
  • Hours: Not verified
  • Type: attraction
  • Tip: A 400-year-old farming village that grows the herbs for Hoi An's kitchens. The hands-on experiences — planting, watering, and cooking with a local family — are the highlight, and a great morning out with kids.

Where to eat

The dishes this article names, at the places locals point to:

Mi Quang Ba Mua

  • Address: 95 Nguyen Chi Thanh Street, Hai Chau District, Da Nang
  • Google Maps: Open in Google Maps
  • Price: About 40,000 VND (~$1.50) per bowl
  • Hours: Not verified
  • Type: restaurant
  • Tip: Order mi quang thit bo (beef) or mi quang tom thit (shrimp and pork). Add the rice crackers on the table — they are meant to be crumbled into the bowl.

Bun Cha Ca 109

  • Address: 109 Nguyen Chi Thanh Street, Hai Chau District, Da Nang
  • Google Maps: Open in Google Maps
  • Price: About 35,000 VND (~$1.35) per bowl
  • Hours: Not verified
  • Type: restaurant
  • Tip: The fish cakes are made fresh in-house. Eat it the local way: squeeze in the lime, add chilli to taste, and eat it hot and fast.

Banh Mi Phuong

  • Tip: The banh mi shop Anthony Bourdain made famous. Expect a queue at lunch — it moves fast. Order the classic mixed (thap cam) and eat it standing on the street like everyone else.

Cao Lau Thanh

  • Address: 26 Thai Phien Street, Hoi An, Da Nang
  • Google Maps: Open in Google Maps
  • Price: Not verified
  • Hours: Not verified
  • Type: restaurant

Before you book in Da Nang or Hoi An: ask these in writing

  • Which side of the price am I on? In Hoi An, a room inside the Ancient Town costs 20–30% more than the same quality two blocks out. Decide whether the lanterns outside your window are worth it — for one night, often yes; for a week, rarely.
  • Is the transfer included? Many Hoi An hotels arrange airport pick-up for about the Grab price (300,000–400,000 VND). Ask when you book instead of finding a taxi at arrivals.
  • Can I change the dates? In October–December, a refundable room is worth more than a cheap one. Floods recede in 1–3 days, but a non-refundable booking does not.
  • When does the tailor need me? If you want a suit or a dress made in Hoi An, allow 2–3 days and start on arrival. A flood week can close the tailors entirely, so do not leave the fitting to your last day.

The bottom line: Da Nang or Hoi An?

There is no wrong base here — there is only the wrong base for the trip you want. Beach, nightlife and easy logistics: Da Nang. Lanterns, tailors and slow evenings: Hoi An, at least one night. Four nights or more: split it, Hoi An first, Da Nang last, and let the airport decide your final night. Book the base before the activities — in February–April, the good rooms in both towns go first.
