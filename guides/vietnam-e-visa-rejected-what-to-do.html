Vietnam E-Visa Rejected? What to Do Next (2026)

You applied, you paid, you waited three working days — and the portal says no. Before you do anything else, take a breath and read the exact word on the screen, because most people who think their Vietnam e-visa was rejected were not rejected at all. There are two very different outcomes that look the same at first glance, and they need opposite responses: one is fixed for free inside the same application, the other costs you a new fee and a new wait. This guide tells you which one you have, why it usually happens, and what to do next in each case.

The short answer: open your application on the official portal, evisa.gov.vn, and read the status. If it says "Amendment required", fix only the item it names and send it back — that costs nothing. If it says "Denial", the application is finished: there is no appeal, so work out the cause, fix it, and file a brand-new application with a new $25 (single entry) or $50 (multiple entry) fee. The fee from the refused application is not refunded. And before you pay again, check whether you even need the visa — citizens of the UK, Germany, France, Italy, Spain, Japan, South Korea and a growing list of other countries can enter visa-free for up to 45 days.

First, check what the portal actually says

The official portal is evisa.gov.vn — run by the Immigration Department of Vietnam's Ministry of Public Security, also reachable at thithucdientu.gov.vn. Any other address is an agency, not the government, even if it looks official and charges more. Go to the Search page on the portal with your registration code and check the status. The portal shows six statuses, and only one of them is a refusal:

Status on the portal What it means What to do
Unpaid The fee has not gone through Pay the fee — the 3-working-day clock has not started
Processing No decision yet Wait; the legal processing time is 3 working days
Amendment required Immigration wants something corrected or added Fix only the item named and send it back — free
Acceptance Approved Download the e-visa PDF and print it
Denial Refused This application is finished; fix the cause and file a new one
Document canceled The document was canceled File a new application

Two false alarms are worth ruling out first. A "Payment failed" screen is not a denial: your card transaction did not go through, Immigration never received the fee, and the 3-working-day deadline never started. And "Processing" after three days is not a refusal either — three working days means Vietnamese working days, Monday to Friday excluding public holidays, so an application filed on a Friday is effectively a Wednesday decision, and processing stretches longer around Tet (Lunar New Year, 6 February 2027).

"Amendment required" is not a rejection — fix it free

This is the outcome most people misread as a rejection, and it is good news. "Amendment required" means Immigration wants one thing corrected — most often a new portrait photo or a clearer passport scan — and the status page adds a Reason line saying exactly what to change, plus a link to edit and return the application to the reviewing agency. Change what the Reason line names and nothing else, then send it back quickly. If you ignore it, the application just sits there, stuck.

Fixing an "Amendment required" application costs nothing and needs no new fee. Only a brand-new application after a Denial costs another $25 or $50. Do not file a new application when you see "Amendment required" — you would pay a second fee and restart the three-working-day clock for no reason.

Why e-visas get denied: the 7 usual reasons

Vietnam does not publish a refusal rate, so any percentage you have seen quoted is a guess. What Immigration does publish, and what the guides that track reader reports agree on, is the list of causes. Almost all of them sit in data the portal checks itself:

1. The portrait photo is wrong

The single most common reason. The photo must be a straight-on portrait against a plain white background, with the full face visible, no glasses, no headwear (religious headwear is allowed but must show the full face), not blurry, not cropped too tight, and not a selfie taken at an angle. A photo taken against a coloured wall, in dim light, or scanned from an old print is the classic failure. Take a new one against a white wall in daylight.

2. The passport scan cannot be read

The scan of your passport data page must be sharp, complete and uncropped — every corner visible, including the machine-readable zone (the two lines of characters at the bottom). A phone photo taken at an angle, with glare across the page, or with the edges cut off, fails the system's check. Scan it flat, in good light, or photograph it straight down.

3. Your name does not match your passport

Enter your name exactly as printed in the machine-readable zone of your passport, including middle names and the exact spelling — not the name you normally use, not a shortened version. Hyphens and special characters are a frequent trip-up: if your passport writes the name one way and your application writes it another, the system flags a mismatch.

4. Your passport is too close to expiring

Your passport must have at least 6 months left from your date of entry into Vietnam. This is strictly enforced, and airlines check it at check-in too — a passport with 5 months left can get the e-visa refused and can also get you stopped at the departure airport. If you are under 6 months, renew the passport first, then apply.

5. The wrong entry date or entry point

The e-visa is issued for the dates and the entry point you choose. Check both before you submit: the start date must cover your arrival day, and you must enter through a border gate on the government's list — which includes the airports at Noi Bai in Hanoi, Tan Son Nhat in Ho Chi Minh City and Da Nang. If your flight changed after you applied, the dates on the visa may no longer match your trip.

6. You applied from inside Vietnam

You cannot apply for an e-visa from inside Vietnam. The application must be filed from outside the country. If you are already in Vietnam and your stay is running out, an e-visa is not the route — talk to Immigration about an extension or a sponsor instead.

7. A past overstay, deportation or entry ban

If you overstayed a previous visit, even by a day, or were deported from Vietnam in the last 3 years or made to leave in the last 6 months, future applications are often refused without explanation. The same applies to the entry bans in Article 21 of the immigration law. A new photo changes nothing here — this is the one category where reapplying with the same details will not help.

What to do after a Denial, step by step

There is no formal appeal for an e-visa refusal. The route is to fix the cause and file again, or to enter another way. Work through it in this order:

1. Save the refusal and accept the fee is gone

Download or screenshot the Denial for your records. Then start with the money, because it is gone: the fee is not refunded when an application is refused, and every applicant ticks a box acknowledging that before paying. A second try means a new application, a new $25 or $50 fee, and a new wait for Immigration's decision.

2. Find the cause before you pay again

Do not reapply with the same file. The same form, the same photo and the same answers cost a second fee and give Immigration nothing new to look at — and if nothing changes, the result often does not change either. Compare every field with your passport, character by character. Replace both the photo and the passport scan, not just one of them. Check the 6-month passport rule and the entry date and entry point. The portal documents a Reason line for "Amendment required", but Vietnam publishes nothing on whether a Denial comes with one, so do not wait around for an explanation that may never come — if the cause is still unclear, use the portal's support form (it lists four issue types: wrong application information, payment failed, forgotten registration code, and others).

3. File a new application — once the cause is fixed

There is no waiting period published by the government before you can reapply. When the problem was the photo, the scan, the name or the address, and you fly in a week or more, reapply yourself on evisa.gov.vn with the corrected details and a new fee, and the three-working-day clock starts again. Apply at least 7 to 10 days before you fly — that buffer turns a refusal into a nuisance instead of a canceled trip, and around Tet, apply three weeks ahead.

4. If your flight is in 2 or 3 days, do not rely on the portal alone

A new e-visa takes three working days, and there is no official express service on the portal. If you fly in two or three days and need a visa, contact the Vietnamese embassy or consulate in your home country and apply for a standard visa there — embassies have discretion the e-visa system does not. That is also the route if your case falls in category 7 above and reapplying online keeps failing.

5. Check whether you need a visa at all

Before you pay a second fee, check the visa-free list. Citizens of the UK, Germany, France, Italy, Spain, Japan, South Korea and a growing list of European countries can enter Vietnam without a visa for stays of up to 45 days. If your trip fits inside 45 days and your passport is on the exemption list, you do not need an e-visa at all — the refusal cost you $25, not the trip.

When reapplying will not help

Be honest with yourself before paying again. Reapplying will not help if you were deported from Vietnam in the last 3 years, made to leave in the last 6 months, or are covered by an entry ban — the system will refuse the same person again. In that case, the embassy route is the only one worth trying, and even there, be upfront about the history. Likewise, if your passport has under 6 months left, no new photo fixes it: renew the passport first.

How to avoid this next time: the 5-minute check before you pay

  • Photo: white wall, daylight, straight on, full face, no glasses. Take it today, not from an old album.
  • Passport scan: flat, sharp, all four corners visible, machine-readable zone readable.
  • Name: copy it letter by letter from the machine-readable zone, middle names included.
  • Passport validity: at least 6 months left on your arrival date — check this before anything else.
  • Dates and entry point: start date on or before your arrival day, entry through a listed gate, and apply at least 7 to 10 days before you fly (three weeks around Tet).
  • The site: evisa.gov.vn only. If the address does not end in .gov.vn, it is an agency charging extra — and an agency filing the same wrong photo gets the same refusal, plus its service fee.

Don't confuse the e-visa with the Pre-Arrival Information form

A new e-visa is not the last form. Vietnam also runs a separate Pre-Arrival Information (PAI) pilot: a free online declaration you file at prearrival.immigration.gov.vn within 72 hours before you land, and you show the QR code it gives you at passport control.

  • It is not a visa. It does not replace your e-visa or your visa-free entry, and filing it does not make you eligible to enter.
  • It is voluntary for now. Vietnam's Immigration Department calls it optional, and reporting as of November 2026 confirms there is no law yet requiring it. Some embassy notices word it more strongly, so the safe move is to file it anyway: it is free, takes a few minutes, and can shorten the queue at the five pilot airports.
  • It only covers five airports so far: Tan Son Nhat (Ho Chi Minh City), Noi Bai (Hanoi), Da Nang, Cam Ranh (Nha Trang) and Phu Quoc. If you land anywhere else, there is nothing to file.
  • Only use the government site. prearrival.immigration.gov.vn is free — any site charging a fee for this form is not the official one.

The ministry has proposed making it compulsory, with no date set, so recheck shortly before you travel.

The bottom line

A Vietnam e-visa refusal is usually one of two things, and they could not be more different. "Amendment required" is a free fix inside the same application — change what the Reason line names and send it back. "Denial" is final for that application: the $25 or $50 fee is gone, there is no appeal, and the way back is a new application with the cause genuinely fixed, or a standard visa from an embassy if time is short. Most refusals come from the photo, the passport scan, or a name that does not match the passport — all fixable, all checkable in five minutes before you pay. Apply a week or more before you fly, and a refusal stays a nuisance instead of a canceled trip.
