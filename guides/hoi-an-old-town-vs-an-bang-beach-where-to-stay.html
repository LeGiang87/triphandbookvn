Where to Stay in Hoi An: Old Town or An Bang Beach? (2026)

Hoi An is really two towns pretending to be one. There is the lantern-lit Ancient Town — a UNESCO World Heritage site of narrow lanes from the 15th to 19th centuries, pedestrian-only for much of the day, where everything worth seeing is on foot. And about 4 to 5 km east, there is An Bang Beach, where the soundtrack is waves instead of tour guides and the day runs on sun loungers and seafood grills. Where you sleep decides which one you get, so this is the first decision to make about Hoi An — before you pick a hotel, pick a base.

The short answer: first-timers staying 2 or 3 nights should stay on the edge of the Ancient Town. You walk to dinner, the lanterns, the tailors and the morning market, and you can duck back to your room when the crowds peak after 4pm. Stay at An Bang Beach if the sea is the point of the trip — a longer stay, a beach-first honeymoon, or a family that wants sand every morning — and accept a bicycle or Grab ride every time you want the Old Town. Staying 4 nights or more? Split it: 2 nights in town, then move to the beach.

Staying in the Ancient Town: the first-timer default

This is where most people should start looking. The Ancient Town plus the streets around it — Cam Pho and Minh An — make up the walkable heart of Hoi An. You are steps from the Japanese Covered Bridge, the night market, the Central Market and dozens of tailor shops. The real advantage is not the sights, it is the escape: the old town gets genuinely packed from about 4pm until late, and if you stay right inside it, you can duck back to your room when it gets too much instead of fighting the crowd until dinner.

Three things to know before you book in town. First, there are no hotels inside the pedestrian core itself — what you are actually booking is a room within a 10-minute walk of it, usually on the edge of the Old Town or across the short bridge onto An Hoi Island, which has the same lantern views with noticeably less foot traffic. Second, cars are banned from the main streets (Tran Phu, Nguyen Thai Hoc, Bach Dang) during pedestrian hours, 09:00–11:00 and 15:00–21:30, so your driver will drop you at the edge and you walk the last few minutes with your bags — that is normal, not a problem with the booking. Third, noise: motorbikes still thread through the lanes outside pedestrian hours, and bars along Nguyen Phuc Chu Street play music until late, so ask for a room away from the street if you are a light sleeper.

Staying at An Bang Beach: sand first, town second

An Bang is Hoi An's beach, full stop — Tripadvisor ranked it one of Asia's best beaches (5th, 2024), and on a calm day the water is almost lake-flat. The beachfront strip is lined with restaurants and bars: order food or a smoothie and the sun lounger is free; otherwise expect 50,000–100,000 VND for the day. The village behind the beach is small and sleepy — family-run homestays, a few cafes, seafood grills that cook the morning catch — and it feels a world away from the tour groups in town.

The catch is the distance. An Bang sits about 4 to 5 km east of the Old Town (sources measure from different points, so the number moves a little). Nobody walks it. You will ride a bicycle (20–40 minutes through rice fields, honestly pleasant), take a Grab (50,000–80,000 VND), a taxi (80,000–120,000 VND), or rent a motorbike (about 120,000–200,000 VND a day — you legally need an International Driving Permit with a motorbike endorsement, and a helmet is mandatory). Many An Bang hotels offer a free shuttle into the Old Town: confirm this before booking if you are not planning to rent a bicycle or a scooter.

One season shifts the choice. The sea is rough from October to January — swim only where lifeguards are on duty — and October–November can flood the Old Town streets. February to August is the beach at its best: dry, warm and sunny. If your trip falls in the rainy months, the Old Town base wins by default.

The third option: Cam Chau, between town and beach

If neither extreme fits, look at Cam Chau — the ward of rice fields sandwiched between the Old Town and the beaches. It is the best-value area in Hoi An: quieter than town, closer to the beach than town, and you can cycle to either in about 15–20 minutes. Boutique villas with pools sit among the paddies, and the commute is a flat bicycle ride rather than a car journey. It suits couples and longer stays best, and anyone who wants a pool and quiet nights without giving up either the lanterns or the sea. The trade-off is that you need a bicycle for everything — there is no walking to dinner here either.

Getting between the two areas

The 4–5 km route is flat and easy: follow Hai Ba Trung east from the Old Town, cross the bridge over the Thu Bon River, and continue straight to An Bang. A bicycle costs about 40,000–60,000 VND a day to rent — most hotels include one free, so ask before you pay a shop. Grab works in both areas for cars and motorbikes. Install Grab and Xanh SM before you travel — both run in Hoi An and Da Nang, so you always have a second option when one is surging. If you are arriving from Da Nang Airport, read our guide to the airport-to-Hoi An Grab first: the same 30 km drive, 250,000–350,000 VND for a 4-seat car, ends at either base — just set your actual hotel as the destination, not "Hoi An", or the pin drops in the middle of the Old Town and your driver will call you to ask where you actually are.

Old Town or An Bang? Choose in 3 questions

Answer these three and the choice picks itself:

1. Is this your first time in Hoi An, and are you staying 2 or 3 nights?

Yes — stay on the edge of the Ancient Town. Two or three nights is not enough to justify a daily commute, and the lanterns after dark are the reason you came. No, you have been before, or you are staying a week — An Bang, or split the stay.

2. What does your perfect day look like?

Lanterns, tailors, street food and a night market — Old Town. A slow morning, a swim, lunch with your feet in the sand — An Bang. Both, honestly — split the stay, town first, beach second, so you only move once.

3. When are you travelling?

February to August — either base works, pick by the day you want. October to January — the Old Town, because the sea is rough and the town floods are a real possibility; check conditions with your hotel before you go out.

The bottom line

For a first trip of two or three nights, the answer is the edge of the Ancient Town: the lantern-lit streets, the Japanese Covered Bridge and the river are a walk away, and you never touch a scooter. An Bang Beach suits a longer stay built around the sea, with the town a bicycle or Grab ride away — and if you have the nights, split the stay and get both Hoi Ans instead of choosing one. Either way, book the base that matches the trip you actually want, not the hotel with the prettiest photos: in Hoi An, where you sleep is the trip.
