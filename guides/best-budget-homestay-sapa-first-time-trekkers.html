Best Budget Homestay in Sapa for First-Time Trekkers (2026)

# Best Budget Homestay in Sapa for First-Time Trekkers (2026)

A homestay is the whole point of Sapa for a first-timer. The town is a base; the valleys are the trip. Sleep one night in a family home in Ta Van or Hau Thao and you wake up inside the rice terraces, eat dinner cooked in the family kitchen, and start the next day's trek from the front door — no bus, no meeting point, no rushing back to town before dark. Sleep in town and you get hot showers, Wi-Fi that works and a bed you can book in two minutes, but you ride 20–45 minutes to every trailhead and the valley stays a day trip.

The good news: a budget homestay night in Sapa is genuinely cheap. Dorm beds in the valleys start at about $5, private rooms at village homestays run about $9–18, and many valley rates include dinner and breakfast — which changes the maths completely, because a town room at the same price leaves you buying every meal separately. The trick is knowing what the price covers, and whether to stay in town or in the valley in the first place.

The short answer: first-time trekkers on a first trip should spend one night in town (to arrive, sort gear and sleep properly after the overnight bus or train) and one night in a valley homestay in Ta Van — booked as part of a guided trek, not on its own. The trek bundle is the best deal in Sapa: a 2-day, 1-night guided trek with a homestay night, meals and transfers runs from about $52 per person, and it removes every logistics problem a first-timer faces in the valleys. If you only have one night, make it the valley night. If you hate shared bathrooms and cold rooms, stay in town and trek as a day trip instead — the valley will still be there next time.

The valley homestays: sleep where you trek

Ta Van village sits about 8–10 km south-east of Sapa town in the Muong Hoa Valley, home to Giay families, and it is the homestay capital of Sapa — wooden and stilt houses looking over the terraces, with the classic Lao Chai to Ta Van trek ending at its door. Hau Thao, about 9 km out, is quieter and higher, home to Black Hmong families, with views across the whole valley and up to Fansipan on a clear day. Both beat town for a trekking base, and both are reached the same way: trek in with a guide, or take a taxi or motorbike taxi from town.

Zizi Mekhoo Sapa Homestay — the budget benchmark

The cheapest proper homestay in the valleys, and the one every budget list starts with. Beds from about $5 in a dorm, with private rooms and family rooms as well, in a big family house on a plateau above Hau Thao village (Ban Pho) with terraces on all sides. Six rooms, a shared kitchen and barbecue area, and a terrace with valley views — on a clear day you can see Fansipan from it. The family runs treks and volunteer classes for local children, and the food is honest and fairly priced: the shared family dinner is about 98,000 VND, a noodle soup about 45,000 VND. Expect basic rooms, firm mattresses and no heating — bring a warm layer, because valley nights are cold. The house sits up a steep 500 m road from the drop-off, so ask for help with a heavy bag. Cash only, like everywhere in the villages.

Hmong House Sapa — the best-value valley stay

If Zizi is the budget pick, Hmong House is the comfort pick at nearly the same price: about $18, with a 4.7 rating across 267 Google reviews, in Ta Van village with the valley literally at the door. Private rooms, en-suite bathrooms in some rooms, and a host family that treks with guests. This is the one to book if you want a valley night without giving up a proper bathroom. Book ahead for September–November weekends, when the golden rice brings the crowds.

Phori's House — the self-catering trekking base

A small house in Ta Van village, about $11 a night, best for trekkers who want a kitchen and a quiet base rather than a hosted family dinner. Self-catering suits couples and small groups who like cooking for themselves after a day on the trails, and the house works well as a start and end point for the Muong Hoa Valley walks. Check the listing for what is included — at this price, meals are usually extra.

Tavan Nature Homestay — the cheapest dorms in Ta Van

Dorm beds from about $5 and private rooms around $9, in Ta Van village itself. A simple, friendly base for exploring the hills on your own, with an on-site restaurant where the owner's family gathers — a good place to practise a few words of Vietnamese over dinner. Good for solo travellers who want the valley at the lowest possible price and do not mind simple rooms.

The town homestays: comfort first, valley second

Stay in town if it is your first night after the overnight bus or train, if you want a hot shower and a heater, or if you are only trekking as a day trip. Both picks below are walkable to the Stone Church square, the lake and the night market.

Little View Homestay — the town pick

About $15 a night in the heart of Sapa town, within walking distance of the lake, the church and the main square. Rooms with mountain views, a garden, luggage storage, free Wi-Fi and hosts who arrange treks, transport and village hikes. The best choice for a first-timer who wants a homestay feel — local food, a family host — without leaving town on night one.

VietTrekking Homestay — the views pick

About $15 a night at the far end of Hoang Lien Street, away from the town noise, in a building guests call a "castle in the clouds". A mix of single rooms, a double room and a dorm, so it suits backpackers, couples and families alike, and the cafe is famous for its mountain views — a good place to plan the next day's trek over coffee while the fog lifts.

Book the trek bundle, not just the bed

This is the single most useful piece of advice in this guide. A village homestay booked on its own means arranging transport out there, finding the house, and trekking without a guide on unmarked trails. Bundled into a guided trek, the same night costs little more and the logistics disappear: a 2-day, 1-night guided trek with a homestay night, meals, an English-speaking local guide and transfers runs from about $52 per person. A full-day guided trek with lunch on its own is roughly 500,000–800,000 VND ($19–31) per person, and a homestay night on its own is roughly 200,000–500,000 VND ($8–19) per person including dinner and breakfast — so the bundle is usually cheaper than the parts, and you get the guide.

Book the bundle through your hotel, a licensed trekking operator in town, or a Hmong-run social enterprise such as Sapa O'Chau — and agree the price and what is included before you set off. Always trek with a local guide in the villages: trails are unmarked, and a guide from the village is also how the money reaches the community. Tell your hotel where you are going, wear shoes with grip (trails turn to slick clay after rain), and do not trek alone after heavy rain — landslides and flash floods can close the mountain roads, and a rockfall blocked National Highway 4D at Ta Phin for several hours in May 2026.

Getting there: train, bus, and the last hour up the mountain

There is no airport in Sapa. From Hanoi you take a sleeper bus or limousine van direct to town (5.5–6.5 hours), or an overnight train to Lao Cai followed by a one-hour road transfer up the mountain — the classic option, and the one our sleeper-train guide covers step by step.

Named bus operators include Sapa Express (cabin sleeper with hotel pickup, 400,000–500,000 VND), Interbus Lines (40-bed sleeper and VIP cabin, 330,000–550,000 VND), Green Bus / G8 Open Tour (sleeper, about 330,000–390,000 VND), Eco Sapa (limousine van, 500,000–900,000 VND) and Sao Viet (sleeper, 250,000–380,000 VND). Book through your hotel, a travel agency or a booking platform a day ahead in peak season; overnight buses save a hotel night, day buses let you watch the mountains appear.

Trains leave Hanoi station in the evening (around 21:45–22:40) and arrive in Lao Cai early in the morning (around 05:20–06:27). A soft sleeper berth runs from about 475,000 VND per person; shared vans wait outside Lao Cai station for each arriving train and cost about 60,000–100,000 VND per person up to Sapa, or about 450,000–550,000 VND for a taxi. Train numbers and times changed in October 2026, so always check the current timetable on dsvn.vn before you book.

Install Grab and Xanh SM (Green SM) before you travel — both operate in Lao Cai province, though cars can be scarce on foggy evenings. A short ride inside Sapa town is usually 30,000–60,000 VND. And withdraw cash in town before heading into the valleys: homestays, guides and market stalls take cash only, and there are no ATMs out there.

When to go, and what to pack for a homestay night

The best months are September to November, when the rice ripens gold and the skies clear after the rains, and March to May, when the weather is mild and the valleys are green. December to February is cold and foggy — atmospheric, but you may see nothing from the viewpoints, and valley nights drop near freezing. June to August is warm and wet, with sudden downpours, leeches on the trails and the highest landslide risk.

Pack for a valley night the way you would for a mountain hut, not a hotel: a warm fleece and a waterproof jacket (mornings can be 10°C and afternoons 25°C), walking shoes with grip, sunscreen, insect repellent, a small dry bag for your phone, and cash in small notes (10,000–50,000 VND) for tips, snacks and the village entrance fee — Ta Van charges 40,000 VND per person, and the Muong Hoa Valley entrance is 75,000 VND per adult. Remove your shoes at the door of a homestay, ask before photographing people — especially children and elders — and do not enter a home or a sacred area uninvited.

Which homestay should you book? Choose in 3 questions

1. Is this your first trek, and do you want a guide to handle everything?

Yes — book the 2-day, 1-night trek bundle with a valley homestay night (Zizi Mekhoo or Hmong House are the usual bases). You get the guide, the meals, the transfers and the night in one price, from about $52. No — if you have trekked in Asia before and just want a cheap bed, book a dorm in Ta Van (Tavan Nature, from $5) and walk the marked valley paths on your own by day.

2. Do you need a private bathroom and heating?

Yes — stay in town (Little View or VietTrekking, about $15) and trek as a day trip, or stretch to Hmong House in the valley, which has en-suite rooms. No — any valley dorm will do, and Zizi Mekhoo at $5 is the benchmark.

3. How many nights do you have in Sapa?

One night — make it the valley night, booked with a guided trek, and arrive by day bus so you start trekking the same afternoon. Two nights — one in town to arrive and recover, one in the valley to wake up in the terraces. Three nights — add a second valley night in Ta Phin for the Red Dao herbal bath (about 150,000–200,000 VND), and a morning at Fansipan if the sky is clear.

The bottom line

For a first-time trekker, the best budget homestay in Sapa is not a single house — it is a pattern: one night in town, one night in the valley, booked as a guided trek bundle. If you want one name, start with Zizi Mekhoo in Hau Thao for the cheapest real valley night (dorms from $5, family dinner about 98,000 VND), or Hmong House in Ta Van if you want an en-suite room and a 4.7-star host family for about $18. Either way, book the trek with the bed, bring cash, pack a warm layer, and let the valley do the rest — the terraces at sunrise from a homestay terrace are the reason you came to Sapa, and no hotel in town can give you that.
