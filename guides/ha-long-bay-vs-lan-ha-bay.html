Ha Long Bay vs Lan Ha Bay: Which Cruise Should a First-Timer Book?

If you are planning your first trip to northern Vietnam, this is probably the first decision you hit: the famous Ha Long Bay, or its quieter neighbour Lan Ha Bay? They are the same limestone seascape — Lan Ha is the southern end of it, off Cat Ba Island — and the line between them is administrative, not geological. What changes is the crowd, the caves, the beaches, and the price.

The short answer: if this is your first time and you want the postcard — the big caves, the viewpoint island, the picture you have seen since childhood — book Ha Long Bay. If you would rather swim, kayak and hear your own paddle instead of fifty other boats, book Lan Ha Bay. Either way, book two days and one night. A day trip gives you about four hours on the water for twelve hours away from Hanoi; one night on the boat is the trip.

Ha Long Bay vs Lan Ha Bay: side-by-side comparison

Ha Long Bay Lan Ha Bay
The scenery The icon. Nearly 2,000 limestone islands, the classic postcard view. The same karsts, greener, with small sandy beaches between them.
Crowds Busy. Several hundred licensed cruise boats; the famous stops bottleneck at midday. Much quieter. A fraction of the boats, spread over more anchorages.
Caves The biggest ones: Sung Sot (Surprise) Cave, the largest in the bay, and Thien Cung on the quieter routes. The signature one: Dark & Bright Cave (Hang Sáng Tối) — paddle by kayak through a low tunnel into a hidden lagoon. Check the itinerary lists it; the Dark side is often closed at high tide. Plus floating fishing villages.
Beaches & swimming One small beach, Ti Top Island — crowded when several boats arrive at once. The reason to come here: swimmable beaches you can reach straight from the boat.
Kayaking A stop on the route, usually at Luon Cave. Part of the day, not a photo stop — sheltered water, kayaks as standard.
Where you board Tuan Chau Marina or Ha Long International Cruise Port, 2.5–3 hours from Hanoi. Usually Got Pier in Hai Phong, about 2 hours from Hanoi, or from Cat Ba Island via Ben Beo harbour.

Ha Long or Lan Ha: choose in 3 questions

Answer these three and the bay picks itself:

1. Do you want the famous sights, or the quiet water?

Famous sights — Sung Sot Cave, Ti Top Island, the Kissing Rocks — means Ha Long. Quiet water, beaches and kayaking means Lan Ha.

2. How much do crowds bother you?

If a queue at a cave entrance would spoil the day, go Lan Ha. If you can shrug and go with the flow of your cruise group, Ha Long is fine.

3. How many nights do you have?

Two nights or fewer, and Ha Long's shorter, simpler logistics win. Three or more, and Lan Ha plus a day on Cat Ba Island is the better use of them — you get the bay and the island in one trip.

Ha Long Bay cruise: one night or two?

One night is the sweet spot for almost everyone: sunset and sunrise among the karsts, a cave, a kayak, a fishing village, and a good night's sleep on the water. Two nights is the only way to reach the genuinely remote water without rushing — and on Ha Long the official overnight fee only rises from 550,000 to 750,000 VND for the second night, the smallest upgrade on the whole trip. A day cruise only makes sense if you are short on nights and treating the bay as a box to tick.

Before you book your Ha Long cruise: ask these in writing

  • Which pier do I board at? Tuan Chau and Ha Long International Cruise Port are in different places; Got Pier is in another city entirely. Confirm it before you book the transfer.
  • Is the sightseeing fee included? A low headline price sometimes hides the fee, added at the pier.
  • What is included — kayak, meals, transfer? Most overnight cruises include the transfer from Hanoi, meals, kayaking and the fee. Budget boats sometimes charge kayaks as an extra.
  • What happens if the cruise is cancelled? Typhoon season runs roughly June to September, and the port authority stops departures when a storm is forecast — usually decided 24 to 48 hours before sailing. Book a cruise with free cancellation and keep a spare day in your plan. See our Seasonal Hazards & Natural Dangers page for the full picture.

The bottom line: Ha Long or Lan Ha?

There is no wrong bay here — there is only the wrong bay for the trip you want. First time, want the postcard, two nights or fewer: Ha Long. Want to swim and kayak without the crowds, or have three nights to spare: Lan Ha, via Cat Ba. Book the cruise before you book the hotel — the best boats sell out on weekends, and the cruise is the trip.
