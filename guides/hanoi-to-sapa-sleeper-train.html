How to Book the Hanoi–Sapa Sleeper Train (and Not Miss It)

There is no train to Sa Pa. That is the first thing to know, and the thing most first-timers get wrong. The overnight train from Hanoi goes to Lao Cai, a border town 35–38 km from Sa Pa, and the last leg is an hour by road up the mountain. Book the train right and you wake up in the mountains having saved a hotel night. Book it wrong and you are standing on a dark platform at 5:20 in the morning holding a voucher nobody will honour.

The short answer: take one of the three night trains — SP15 at 21:45, SP3 at 22:00 or SP7 at 22:40 — and sleep in a 4-berth soft sleeper. A berth in Vietnam Railways' own carriage costs from about 475,000 VND (~$18); a private 4-berth cabin costs about 900,000–1,200,000 VND (~$35–46) per berth. Book 10–14 days ahead in the busy season (September–November). And check one thing before you pay: are you buying a ticket, or a voucher you still have to exchange at the station?

Since 10 October 2026 the line runs three night-train pairs instead of two, and the old SP1/SP2 numbers that most guides still quote now run only on extra holiday schedules. If a listing tells you about "SP1 at 21:35", its timetable is out of date.

How to book the Hanoi–Sapa sleeper train, step by step

1. Pick your cabin first, then your booking channel

Decide the cabin before you open a booking site — the channel depends on it. Vietnam Railways' own berths are the cheapest and sell out first. Private cabins cost more but are usually still available when the state berths are gone, and they are the only way to get a 2-berth VIP cabin.

2. Book on the official site, or on a site that takes foreign cards

Vietnam Railways sells tickets on dsvn.vn, which has an English version. The name on the ticket must match your passport exactly. The official site often rejects foreign bank cards — if your payment fails there, that is normal, not a sign the train is full. Baolau and 12Go both take international cards and sell the private carriages too; Klook also lists the route. Your hotel in Hanoi can book for you as well, usually for a small fee, and that is the easiest option if you are already staying in the Old Quarter.

3. Book early in the busy season

Tickets usually go on sale about two months ahead. In the peak months of September–November, book 10–14 days ahead; in the low season 2–3 days is usually enough. For Tet, book 4 or more weeks ahead. Weekend trains sell out before weekday ones, and the cheapest Vietnam Railways berths are always the first to go.

4. Check what you actually bought: ticket or voucher?

This is the trap most guides never answer plainly — so here it is. Some private-cabin sellers send you a voucher, not a ticket. A voucher is a promise, not a seat: you must exchange it for a real ticket at Hanoi station before boarding, which means arriving 60 minutes early instead of 30, and finding the right counter. Before you pay, ask the seller in writing: "Is this an e-ticket I can board with, or a voucher I must exchange at the station?" Also ask whether the Lao Cai–Sa Pa transfer is included — many train tickets cover the rail part only, and end at Lao Cai station.

Where to board: Gate B, not the main gate

Trains to Lao Cai board from the back entrance of Hanoi Railway Station, Gate B on Tran Quy Cap street — not the main gate at 120 Le Duan. First-timers regularly go to the wrong gate and then run around the block with their bags. Arrive 30 minutes early, or 60 minutes if you have a voucher to exchange. The station has a waiting room and small shops; buy water and snacks before you board, because there is no dining car worth planning around.

On board: what the night is actually like

A 4-berth soft sleeper is a small lockable cabin with two bunks on each side, clean bedding and air conditioning. The bottom bunks are easier to get in and out of and cost a little more; the top bunks have less headroom. There is no shower. The toilet is at the end of the carriage — bring your own tissues and hand sanitiser. The train is not silent: it rocks, it brakes, and the corridor light leaks under the door. Bring earplugs and an eye mask. Keep your passport, money and phone on your person or under your pillow, not in a bag on the floor. Luggage goes under the bottom bunk or on the rack above the door; big suitcases are awkward in a 4-berth cabin, so pack a soft bag if you can. The conductor wakes the carriage before Lao Cai, around 05:00 — set your own alarm too, because the train does not wait.

Arriving in Lao Cai at dawn: the last hour to Sa Pa

You arrive between 05:20 and 06:27, in the dark in winter and in the half-light in summer. Lao Cai is not Sa Pa — it is a working border town 35–38 km away, and the road up takes about 50 minutes to an hour. Shared vans wait outside the station for each arriving train: about 60,000–100,000 VND (~$2–4) per person. A taxi for the whole car is about 450,000–550,000 VND (~$17–21) — agree the price before you get in, and confirm it is for the car, not per person. Some hotels and private-cabin sellers include the transfer; if yours does, find their sign at the exit instead of taking a van. Check in Sa Pa is usually 14:00, so ask your hotel in advance about an early check-in or at least a place to leave your bags — you will arrive with most of the day still ahead of you.

Train or sleeper bus? Choose in 3 questions

Answer these three and the choice picks itself:

1. Do you get carsick, or hate sleeping on buses?

If yes, take the train. The ride is smoother, you can stand up and walk around, and the bunks are real beds. If no, the bus is simpler — it goes straight to Sa Pa town with no transfer, in 5.5–6.5 hours, and costs about the same as the cheapest train berth.

2. Who is travelling with you?

Solo and on a budget: a Vietnam Railways 4-berth berth, booked early. A couple who want privacy: a VIP 2-berth cabin, or book all four berths of a private cabin. A family of four: book a whole private 4-berth cabin — the price per person is close to four separate berths, and the door locks.

3. When are you going?

September–November is the busy season: the train needs booking 10–14 days ahead, and if the cheap berths are gone, the bus is the fallback that still gets you there the same night. In the low season you can often book the train 2–3 days ahead and still get the cabin you want.

Before you book: ask these in writing

  • Ticket or voucher? If it is a voucher, where exactly do I exchange it, and how early must I arrive?
  • Is the Lao Cai–Sa Pa transfer included? If not, what does the van cost and where do I find it?
  • Which train number, and what time? SP15, SP3 or SP7 — and is that the time on my ticket, not on an old blog?
  • Private cabin or shared berth? If I book one berth in a 4-berth cabin, who else sleeps in it?
  • What if the train is cancelled? Rainy-season closures happen — who refunds me, and how fast?

The bottom line: the Hanoi–Sapa sleeper train

The train is the classic way to Sa Pa for a reason: you sleep through the dull part and wake up in the mountains. But it is not the simplest way — the bus is simpler, and the train only earns its keep if you book the right cabin, on the right train, with a real ticket. Get those three right and it is the best night's travel in northern Vietnam. Get the voucher wrong and the trip starts with a 5:20 argument on a cold platform.
