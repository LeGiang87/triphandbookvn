Vietnam SIM Card or eSIM: Which to Buy Before You Land (2026)

You land in Vietnam, your phone says "No Service", and the Grab driver you need to get into town is an app you cannot open. That first hour is the whole reason to sort out your connection before you fly. The good news is that Vietnam has some of the cheapest mobile data in the world — a tourist SIM with 5 GB a day costs about $2.50 for a week, and an eSIM bought online costs from $10 and works the moment you land. The bad news is that the airport kiosk will happily sell you the same thing for three times the price, and the "unlimited" eSIM you bought may slow to a crawl after 3 GB a day.

This guide compares the two options honestly: a physical SIM from Viettel, VinaPhone or MobiFone against a travel eSIM from Airalo, Saily, Ubigi, Holafly or GigSky — with real prices checked in November 2026, where to buy each one, and the traps to avoid.

The short answer: staying under a week and only need data? Buy an eSIM before you fly — Airalo's 5 GB / 7-day plan is $10, or Saily's 10 GB / 30-day plan is $17.99 — and you have internet the second the plane doors open. Staying longer, heading to the mountains, or need a Vietnamese phone number? Buy a Viettel SIM in a shop in town: the 5G60T plan is 60,000 VND (~$2.50) for 7 days at 5 GB a day, and Viettel has the best coverage outside the cities. Only buy at the airport if you need data immediately — the same SIM costs 200,000–350,000 VND at an airport counter, roughly three times the in-town price. Whatever you buy, check that data works before you leave the counter, and install Grab and Xanh SM before you travel — you will need data to call a car from the airport.

The choice is not really SIM versus eSIM — it is when you want to be connected. An eSIM means you step off the plane already online. A physical SIM means you queue at a counter first, but you get a real Vietnamese number and the cheapest data in the country. Many travellers do both: an eSIM for the first day, then a Viettel SIM in town for the rest of the trip.

The three networks: Viettel vs VinaPhone vs MobiFone

Vietnam has three big carriers, and they are not equal once you leave the cities. Viettel is the largest and has the best coverage, especially in the mountains — Sapa, Ha Giang and the northern highlands. VinaPhone is strong in the cities and weaker in the mountains. MobiFone is strongest in Ho Chi Minh City and the Mekong Delta. If your trip is Hanoi, Ha Long Bay and Hoi An, any of the three works. If you are trekking in Sapa, buy Viettel.

Two things to know before you pay. First, the SIM card itself and the data package are often priced separately — the SIM alone costs about 50,000 VND at some counters, and a Viettel prepaid SIM is about 100,000 VND, so ask for the total before you hand over cash. Second, VinaPhone's tourist SIM cannot be topped up — when the 15 days or the data runs out, you buy a new one. Viettel and MobiFone packages can be renewed in town.

Prices move between counters and change over time. The numbers above are what the carriers charged in November 2026 — confirm the price at the counter before you pay, and if the counter price differs from this guide, the counter price is the one that counts.

Travel eSIMs compared

A travel eSIM is a data plan from an international provider that rides on a Vietnamese network — usually VNPT (VinaPhone). You buy it online, scan a QR code, and it activates when you land. No passport, no queue, no Vietnamese number. These were the prices in November 2026:

Read the word "unlimited" carefully. Airalo's unlimited plans slow to 1 Mbps after 3 GB in a day. Saily slows to 1 Mbps after 5 GB in a day. Holafly gives you unlimited data on the phone itself, but hotspot use is capped at 1 GB a day — so if you planned to share the connection with a laptop, that cap is the real limit. For most travellers, a fixed 10 GB plan is better value than an "unlimited" one: 10 GB covers maps, Grab, messaging and a lot of video for a week, and it costs half as much.

Compare all of this with roaming on your home carrier. AT&T and Verizon charge $12 a day for international roaming in Vietnam — $120 for a 10-day trip, for the same data a $13–18 eSIM gives you. Turn roaming off before you land unless you have checked your carrier's Vietnam rate in writing.

Where to buy: the airport or in town?

This is where most first-timers overpay. SIM counters sit in the arrivals hall of every international airport — Noi Bai Terminal 2 in Hanoi, Tan Son Nhat in Ho Chi Minh City, and Da Nang — right after customs, and the staff activate the SIM on the spot. That convenience has a price: a short tourist SIM at an airport kiosk costs about 200,000–350,000 VND, and one Viettel price board photographed at an airport kiosk started at 630,000 VND a month (~$24), while a Viettel shop in central Hanoi sold a bigger package for about 210,000 VND (~$8).

The rule is simple. If you need data the moment you land — a late-night arrival, a Grab to book, a hotel to find — buy at the airport and accept the markup. If you can wait an hour, buy in town: any official Viettel, VinaPhone or MobiFone shop sells the same packages for roughly a third of the airport price, and the staff there do this all day. Do not buy a SIM from a tout in the arrivals hall or from a "free SIM" bundled with a tour — the free ones are usually registered to someone else, and a SIM registered to a stranger can be cut off without warning.

Registration: a physical SIM in Vietnam must be registered with your passport, under Decree 163/2024 — the shop photographs your passport and activates the SIM in your name. Your passport must be valid for the whole stay, matching your visa. An eSIM bought online before you fly needs no passport at all.

One thing that is still unclear: one travel site reported in November 2026 that a rule from April 2026 requires SIM registration to be verified again through the VNeID app — but the official guidance does not say how a visitor without a VNeID account is supposed to do that. Airport counters are still selling and activating SIMs on a passport as normal, so do not expect to be refused a SIM. Treat it as one more reason to have an eSIM or roaming ready before you fly, not as a reason to panic.

How to set it up

eSIM — do this before you fly. Buy the plan online, scan the QR code the provider emails you, and install the eSIM while you still have home Wi-Fi. Do not activate the data plan yet — set it to start when you land, or turn it on when the plane touches down. Check first that your phone supports eSIM at all: iPhone XR and newer, Pixel 3 and newer, Samsung Galaxy S20 and newer, and most phones sold since 2020. If your phone is older or was bought locked to a carrier, a travel eSIM will not install — buy a physical SIM instead.

Physical SIM — do this at the counter. Hand over your passport, pick a package, and let the staff put the SIM in your phone and activate it. Before you walk away, check three things: mobile data is on, a web page loads, and you can see the data balance. If the counter has a data-check code, ask the staff to run it with you — the one minute this takes is worth more than the walk back to the airport the next day. Keep your home SIM somewhere safe; a small zip bag in your passport holder works.

Once you are online: install Grab and Xanh SM before you travel — both are in the app stores, and the fare shows in the app before you book, so there is no haggling. You need mobile data to call a car from the airport, which is exactly why a SIM is a first-hour purchase, not a second-day errand. Pitfalls and scams to watch for

The airport markup. The same SIM costs two to three times more at the airport than in town. Buy there only if you need data now.

The "free" SIM. A SIM handed out free with a tour or a hotel is usually registered to someone else. It can stop working at any time, and you cannot top it up in your own name. Pay for your own.

The "unlimited" that is not. Check the daily cap before you buy an unlimited eSIM. Airalo slows after 3 GB a day, Saily after 5 GB, and Holafly caps hotspot sharing at 1 GB a day. A 10 GB fixed plan is usually better value.

The 2G phone. Vietnam switched off 2G completely on 15 September 2026. A phone that only supports 2G will not connect at all — and some very old 3G-only phones struggle in rural areas. If your phone is more than eight years old, check it supports 4G before you fly.

The VinaPhone top-up trap. VinaPhone's tourist SIM cannot be topped up. If you buy it for 15 days and run out of data on day 10, you buy a new SIM. If you think you will use more than 1 GB a day, pick Viettel or MobiFone instead.

Roaming left on. Turn off data roaming on your home SIM before you land, or your phone may quietly connect to a Vietnamese network at $12 a day. If you use a dual-SIM phone, set the Vietnamese SIM as the data line and keep your home SIM for calls and texts only.

Which one should you buy? Choose in 3 questions

1. How long are you staying?

Under a week and only need data — buy an eSIM before you fly (Airalo 5 GB for $10, or Saily 10 GB for $17.99). Two weeks or more — buy a Viettel SIM in town; 150,000 VND (~$6) covers 30 days at 5 GB a day.

2. Are you going to the mountains?

Yes — Sapa, Ha Giang, the northern highlands — buy Viettel, in town. It has the best coverage where the other networks fade. No — cities and the coast only — any carrier works, and an eSIM on the VNPT network is fine.

3. Do you need a Vietnamese number?

Yes — for a local bank app, a hotel that calls you, or a tour operator who needs to reach you — buy a physical SIM, or the Viettel Tourist eSIM, the only eSIM here with a +84 number. No — buy any travel eSIM and save the queue.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a passport to buy a SIM?

For a physical SIM, yes — registration with a passport is mandatory. For an eSIM bought online before you fly, no.

Does an eSIM give me a Vietnamese phone number?

Usually no — travel eSIMs are data only. The exception is Viettel's official Tourist eSIM, which comes with a +84 number.

Can I use Grab with a tourist SIM or eSIM?

Yes — Grab and Xanh SM work on any data connection. Install both apps before you travel, and make sure your data works before you leave the SIM counter, because you will need it to call a car from the airport.

What happens when I run out of data?

Viettel and MobiFone packages can be topped up at any official shop or through the carrier's app. VinaPhone's tourist SIM cannot be topped up — buy a new one. Travel eSIMs can usually be topped up in the provider's app, but check before you buy.

Will my phone work in Vietnam?

Any phone that supports 4G will work. Check eSIM support (iPhone XR+, Pixel 3+, Samsung S20+) before buying an eSIM, and remember 2G was switched off on 15 September 2026.

Is airport Wi-Fi enough instead of a SIM?

For the first hour, maybe — but airport Wi-Fi drops the moment you leave the terminal, and you need data for Grab, maps and messages on the road. A SIM is a first-hour purchase, not a nice-to-have.

The bottom line

For a first-timer, the simplest setup is an eSIM bought before you fly — $10–18, working the moment you land — and then a Viettel SIM from a shop in town if you stay longer than a week, head for the mountains, or need a Vietnamese number. Buy at the airport only if you need data immediately, and expect to pay two to three times the in-town price for the convenience. Whatever you buy, check that data works before you leave the counter, turn roaming off on your home SIM, and install Grab and Xanh SM before you travel. The first hour in Vietnam should be spent on the road, not in a phone shop.
