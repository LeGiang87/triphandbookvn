Hoi An Lantern-Making Class for Beginners: How to Book (2026)

Every evening in Hoi An, hundreds of silk lanterns light up the Old Town — and the best souvenir in town is not one you buy, it is one you make. A lantern-making class takes one to two hours, costs about $8–16, and ends with a foldable silk lantern you made yourself, packed flat in your suitcase. No craft skills needed: the teachers do this with first-timers all day, every day, and children from about six manage fine.

The catch is that not every "class" in Hoi An is the same experience. Some workshops hand you a ready-made bamboo frame and you just glue silk around it — quick, easy, and done in under an hour. Others teach you to build the frame from split bamboo yourself, which is the real craft and takes the full 90 minutes to two hours. Both are fun, but they are different products at different prices, and the listings do not always say which one you are buying. This guide tells you which is which, what each costs, and how to book the one that matches the afternoon you actually want.

The short answer: book the standard full class, not the express one, if you have 90 minutes — you build the frame yourself, and that is the part you will remember. Budget about 250,000–400,000 VND ($10–16) per person, or about $8–10 if you book the same class through GetYourGuide. Book online a day ahead in high season, especially for evening slots, because they pair naturally with a lantern-lit stroll afterwards and they sell out first. Walk in only if you are flexible about the time — and if a listing adds a "government fee" of 100,000 VND per person on top of the price, that is a combo tour, not a plain class: check what is included before you pay.

What actually happens in a class

A real lantern starts long before you sit down. The bamboo in the frame is old — at least five years old, because younger bamboo warps as it dries and will not hold a curve. Workshops boil it, then soak it in salt water for about ten days to keep the borer beetles out, then sun-dry it, split it and shave it into ribs. None of that is your job. Your job starts when the teacher hands you the ribs.

Here is the usual flow, step by step:

  1. Pick your shape and silk. The classic shapes are garlic-bulb, diamond and globe, plus lotus and triangle at some workshops. Silk comes in the classic reds and golds, or whatever the workshop has in stock that week — brighter colours photograph better at dusk, darker ones glow warmer with a bulb inside.
  2. Build the frame. In a full class you bend and bind the bamboo ribs into shape yourself, with the teacher correcting your hands as you go. This is the slow part — about half the class — and the part that makes it feel like a craft rather than a colouring-in exercise. In an express class this step is skipped: the frame is ready and waiting.
  3. Stretch and glue the silk. You cut the silk to size, glue it panel by panel around the frame, and trim the edges. The teacher shows you how to keep it taut without tearing it — the one skill that takes a minute to learn and makes the difference between a lantern and a lopsided balloon.
  4. Finish and fold. Tassels, a hanging loop, and then the teacher shows you how to fold the finished lantern flat for your bag. This last step matters more than it sounds: a silk lantern that cannot fold is a souvenir you will crush in your suitcase on day two.

Total time: 45–60 minutes for an express class, 90 minutes to two hours for a full class. Everything is included in the price — materials, tools, the teacher, and the lantern you take home. Most classes also include tea, and a few throw in a small extra gift from the host.

The Lantern Lady — the cheapest real class

Thuy, "the Lantern Lady", teaches in a small workshop at 3/6 Phan Boi Chau, a short walk from the Old Town. Classes run 100,000–200,000 VND at the door for about two hours — the cheapest proper class in town — and the same workshop is the meeting point for the $8 GetYourGuide listing (4.9 stars, 410 reviews), which makes a foldable 20 cm lantern in about one hour. Small groups, an English-speaking teacher, and a hidden-gem feel that the bigger workshops have lost. This is the class to pick if you want the lowest price and do not mind a no-frills room. Book the online listing if you want a fixed start time; walk in if you are flexible — but note the workshop closes at 20:00, so the last class starts well before that.

Lantern Making Class with Lynn — the evening option

Lynn's studio at 57 Dao Duy Tu sits about 350 metres from the Japanese Covered Bridge, and it is the only workshop in this guide that stays open late: daily 09:00–22:30, with the last full class starting at 21:00 and the last express class at 21:30. Two options: the Express Silk Class (250,000 VND, 45–60 minutes, decorate a prepared frame) and the Full Bamboo Masterclass (350,000 VND, about 90 minutes, build the frame first). Walk-ins are welcome when space is available, and the air-conditioned room is a genuine relief on a hot Hoi An afternoon. Pick Lynn if your day is already full and you want a class after dinner — it is the only one that fits a 21:00 start.

Hoi An Handicraft Tours — the highest-reviewed class

The $10 GetYourGuide listing run by Hoi An Handicraft Tours is the highest-reviewed craft class in Hoi An by a wide margin: 4.9 stars across 3,742 reviews. Ninety minutes to two hours, a welcome drink of traditional Vietnamese tea, an English-speaking instructor with a translator, a foldable lantern to take home, and a small "secret gift" from the host. The full two-hour version on Viator ($15.42) meets at 507 Hai Ba Trung and adds a second lantern — one made by your instructor — plus a herbal tea tasting. Pick this one if you want the safest booking: the most reviews, a fixed meeting point, and a price that includes everything.

Reaching Out Arts & Crafts — the one with a purpose

Reaching Out is a fair-trade social enterprise that employs artisans with disabilities, mostly deaf and physically disabled craftspeople, in a century-old merchant shophouse at 103 Nguyen Thai Hoc. Its lantern workshop is different from the others: you make a foldable diamond-shaped lantern from handmade do bark paper with elegant patterns, rather than the usual silk-and-bamboo version. The price at the counter was not verified for this guide — ask on arrival or book through their website — but the experience is quieter, slower and more personal than the tourist workshops, and the attached Tea House is staffed entirely by deaf employees (you order by placing wooden blocks in a tray). Even if you do not take the class, the shop is worth a visit for handmade ceramics, silk and jewellery. Pick Reaching Out if the story behind the souvenir matters to you as much as the souvenir itself.

Hoi An Handicraft Workshop — watch first, then decide

Not a class, but the best place to see the craft before you pay for it. The Handicraft Workshop at 9 Nguyen Thai Hoc puts twelve crafts under one roof — lanterns, pottery, woodwork, embroidery and mats — with artisans working at them in front of you, plus short bai choi folk shows at 10:15, 15:15 and 16:15. It is one of the 22 Old Town ticket sites, so entry costs one 20,000 VND ticket, and it is open 08:00–17:30. Go in the morning, watch a lantern being built, and then decide whether you want the full class or the express one — you will know exactly what you are buying.

How to book (and what to watch for)

Book online a day ahead in high season. Evening slots sell out first because they pair with a lantern-lit walk afterwards, and the most-reviewed listings fill up on weekends. GetYourGuide and Viator both sell the same workshops — compare the two, because the same class can be $8 on one and $15 on the other, and the inclusions differ (the $15 version adds a second lantern and a tea tasting).

Walk-ins work, with limits. Lynn's studio takes walk-ins until 21:00–21:30, and The Lantern Lady takes walk-ins during the day. But a walk-in gets whatever slot is free, not the slot you want — and on a busy evening, that can mean no slot at all.

Check what the price includes. A plain class includes the instructor, all materials and the lantern you take home. Some combo listings add extras that cost extra: one lantern-making-plus-boat-trip combo ($18.47) adds a 100,000 VND per person "government fee" that is not in the headline price, and one acrylic-painting workshop charges 50% extra on Vietnamese public holidays, payable in cash at the meeting point. Read the "what's included" line before you pay, not after.

Do not confuse making with buying. A finished lantern at the night market costs about 50,000–150,000 VND and takes two minutes of haggling. If all you want is a lantern on your wall at home, buy one — it is cheaper than any class. The class is worth it for the making, not the object.

Kids are welcome. Children from about six manage fine, and the express class suits them best — shorter, simpler, and the frame is already built, so they get to the fun part (choosing silk and gluing) straight away. Under-sixes will struggle with the glue and the waiting; take them to watch at the Handicraft Workshop instead.

Getting there: Hoi An has no station and no airport

Every lantern workshop in this guide sits inside or just outside the walkable Old Town, so once you are in Hoi An, you walk. Getting to Hoi An is the part that needs planning: the town has no airport and no train station. Fly into Da Nang Airport, then take a Grab car 30 km to Hoi An (250,000–350,000 VND for a 4-seat car — our airport-to-Hoi An guide covers the pickup zone and the tout trap). By train, the nearest station is Da Nang, about 30 km away, with the same Grab ride at the end.

Install Grab and Xanh SM before you travel — both run in Hoi An and Da Nang, so you always have a second option when one is surging. And remember the Old Town is pedestrian-only for much of the day: cars are banned from the main streets (Tran Phu, Nguyen Thai Hoc, Bach Dang) from 09:00–11:00 and 15:00–21:30, so your driver will drop you at the edge and you walk the last few minutes. That is normal, not a problem — and the walk in is the best part, because the lanterns are already lit.

Taking your lantern home

The whole point of a Hoi An lantern is that it folds flat. Your teacher will show you how to collapse it — do not skip this step, and practise it once in the workshop while someone can correct you. Pack it flat in your carry-on between soft clothes, not in checked luggage under a pile of shoes. A 20 cm lantern fits in a daypack; a larger one needs a full suitcase slot. If you bought a second lantern at the market, the same rule applies — flat, between clothes, never loose.

At home, hang it where it catches the light but not direct sun — silk fades in strong sunlight over a season. A small LED fairy light inside (battery, never a candle) gives you the Hoi An glow without the fire risk, and it is how most people actually display theirs.

After the class: the lantern evening

A lantern class is best done as the first half of an evening, not a standalone errand. Finish around dusk, then walk the Old Town while the lanterns come on: cross the Japanese Covered Bridge, wander Tran Phu and Nguyen Thai Hoc, and cross to An Hoi Island for the night market. The full-moon lantern night each month is the peak — the electric lights go off around 18:00–22:00 and the town glows by lantern alone; the remaining 2026 date is 22 December.

If you want the floating-lantern moment, take a short boat ride on the Hoai River and release a paper lantern with a wish — agree the price and the length of the ride before you step on board, and buy your floating lantern from the boat vendor, not from touts on the street. Then eat: Hoi An after dark is a food town, and you are already in the middle of it.

Which class should you book? Choose in 3 questions

1. How much time do you have?

Under an hour — the express class at Lynn's (45–60 minutes, 250,000 VND) or the $8 GetYourGuide class at The Lantern Lady (1 hour). Ninety minutes or more — the full class, any of the three main workshops. A whole morning — watch the craft at the Handicraft Workshop first, then take the full class in the afternoon.

2. What matters more: the lowest price, or the most reviews?

Lowest price — The Lantern Lady, 100,000–200,000 VND at the door or $8 online. Most reviews and the safest booking — Hoi An Handicraft Tours, $10 on GetYourGuide (4.9, 3,742 reviews). The story behind the souvenir — Reaching Out, where your money supports artisans with disabilities.

3. Are you booking for the evening?

Yes — Lynn's is the only workshop with classes starting after 20:00 (last full class 21:00, last express 21:30), so book it a day ahead. No — any of the daytime workshops works, and the morning slots are the quietest.

The bottom line

For a first-timer, the best lantern-making class in Hoi An is the full 90-minute class — build the frame, stretch the silk, fold it flat, take it home. Budget 250,000–400,000 VND ($10–16) at the door or $8–10 online, book a day ahead for an evening slot, and ask one question before you pay: "do I build the bamboo frame myself?" If the answer is yes, you are buying the real craft. If the answer is no, you are buying the express version — still fun, still a lovely souvenir, but half the experience for not much less money. Either way, do it before dark, then walk out into the lantern-lit streets carrying something you made. That is the Hoi An evening most people remember.
