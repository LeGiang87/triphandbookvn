A. Overview

Hue is Vietnam's old imperial capital — slower, quieter and more graceful than Hanoi or Ho Chi Minh City. For 143 years the Nguyen emperors ruled from the walled Imperial City on the Perfume River, and their palaces, tombs and pagodas are still here: grand, weathered, and far less crowded than the country's other big sights. Hue is the place to come when you want history, food and river light rather than beaches and nightlife.

Give Hue 2–3 days: one for the Imperial City, one for the royal tombs and Thien Mu Pagoda, and a third for the countryside, the food, or a slow boat on the Perfume River. The best weather is February to August — dry and warm. October and November bring heavy rain and floods; the city can flood badly in those months, so check conditions before you travel then. Hue suits history lovers, food lovers (bun bo Hue was born here), couples, and anyone travelling between Da Nang and the north who wants a gentler stop. Prices checked November 2026.

B. Getting There

By plane. Phu Bai International Airport is about 14–15 km south of the city centre, a 25–30 minute drive. Vietnam Airlines, VietJet Air and Bamboo Airways fly from Hanoi and Ho Chi Minh City. Fares change with the season, so check prices when you book — there is no fixed fare to quote here.

By train. Hue Railway Station is on the main north–south line, a short walk from the city centre. The most popular ride is the train to Da Nang: 2.5–3 hours over the Hai Van Pass, 100,000–250,000 VND (~$4–10). Sit on the sea side (left, going south) for the coastal views. Trains also run to Hanoi and Ho Chi Minh City daily; check schedules and fares on the Vietnam Railways booking site before you travel.

By bus or car. Sleeper buses run overnight from Hanoi (about 12–14 hours) and Ho Chi Minh City (about 18–20 hours). From Da Nang, a limousine van takes 2–3 hours and costs roughly $6–14 per seat; a private car costs about 1,000,000–1,500,000 VND (~$38–58).

C. Getting Around

Hue is compact and flat, and the main sights cluster around the river. Grab works well here for cars and motorbikes, and prices are shown up front. Cyclos (pedicabs) wait around the Imperial City and the tourist streets — agree the price and the route before you get on, and expect to pay by the hour. Bicycles are a lovely way to explore the river road and the tombs; rentals are available at most hotels.

No verified Hue-specific taxi number found — use Grab, or ask your hotel to call a local company for you. A Grab across the city centre usually costs 30,000–60,000 VND (~$1.15–2.30). Motorbike rental costs about 150,000–200,000 VND (~$6–8) per day; you legally need an International Driving Permit with a motorbike endorsement, and a helmet is mandatory.

E. Food & Drink

Hue's food is the most refined in Vietnam — the old imperial court demanded small, beautiful, complex dishes, and the city still cooks that way. Bun bo Hue (spicy beef noodle soup with lemongrass) is the dish the city is famous for. Com hen (clam rice) is the cheap, punchy local lunch. And the city's banh beo (tiny steamed rice cakes) and banh khoai (crispy pancakes) are the snacks to graze on between sights.

Vegetarian and allergies: Hue is one of the best cities in Vietnam for vegetarians, thanks to its Buddhist tradition — many restaurants serve a full vegetarian menu, especially on the 1st and 15th days of the lunar month. G. Sample Itineraries

1 day — the Imperial City. Morning: the Imperial City (arrive at opening, allow 3 hours). Lunch: bun bo Hue at Me Keo or Quan Cam. Afternoon: Thien Mu Pagoda by dragon boat, then Dong Ba Market. Evening: dinner on Le Loi Street and a walk along the riverfront.

2 days — add the tombs. Day 1 as above. Day 2: morning at the Tomb of Tu Duc and the Tomb of Minh Mang, lunch of com hen, afternoon at the Tomb of Khai Dinh, evening at a vegetarian restaurant.

3 days — add the countryside. Day 3: a morning cycling along the river road to the villages, an afternoon at a local cooking class or a spa, and a final sunset boat on the Perfume River.

H. Money & Budget

Prices checked November 2026. 1 USD ≈ 26,000 VND.

  • Backpacker: about 700,000–1,000,000 VND (~$27–38) per day — hostel or budget guesthouse, street food, bicycle, one tomb or the Citadel.
  • Mid-range: about 1,500,000–2,500,000 VND (~$58–96) per day — comfortable hotel, restaurant meals, Grab, the Citadel combo ticket.
  • Comfortable: about 3,500,000 VND+ (~$135+) per day — riverside hotel, private car and driver, boat cruise, fine dining.

ATMs are plentiful on Le Loi Street and around the Citadel; most accept foreign cards and charge 20,000–50,000 VND (~$0.80–1.90) per withdrawal. Cards work in hotels and larger restaurants, but street food, markets, cyclos and boat rides are cash-only. Bring small notes — a 500,000 VND bill is hard to break at a bun bo stall. Currency exchange is available at banks on Le Loi Street; avoid the airport exchange counters, whose rates are poor.

Scams and risks to watch for: Cyclo drivers who agree one price and demand another at the end — agree the price and the route in writing if you can. Boat operators who add "fees" mid-river — agree the full price before boarding. Flooding: October and November bring heavy rain, and Hue floods more than any other city in central Vietnam. If the river is high, check with your hotel before going out, and do not drive through flooded streets.

Seasonal hazards

October and November bring the heaviest rain and the main flood risk — the Perfume River can rise by several metres, with the south bank flooding first. The Hai Van Pass between Hue and Da Nang is foggy in rain and carries a landslide risk after heavy rain; ride it on a clear day, and avoid it from October to December. The best window for Hue is March to August. See the full picture on our Seasonal Hazards & Natural Dangers page.

J. Local Tips

Seasons: February to August is dry and sunny, 28–35°C. September to December is the rainy season; October–November can bring floods that close roads and the Citadel for a day or two. January is cooler (20–25°C) and pleasant.

Culture and etiquette: Dress modestly at pagodas, temples and the Imperial City's inner halls — cover shoulders and knees. Remove shoes when entering a temple hall or someone's home. Hue people are famously polite and soft-spoken; a gentle tone and a smile will get you further than loud bargaining. Pointing with feet is rude; so is touching someone's head.

Bargaining: Expected at Dong Ba Market and with cyclo drivers, not in restaurants or shops with price tags. Stay friendly, start at about half, and accept the deal with a smile.

What to pack: Sunscreen, a hat, comfortable walking shoes (the Citadel is big and the tomb steps are steep), a rain jacket in the wet season, and a light layer for cool January evenings.

N. Local Specialties & Souvenirs

Hue's souvenirs are the gentlest in Vietnam — sesame sweets, sour shrimp and poetry hats — and almost all of them are sold in one place: Dong Ba Market (Place block in Section D), the city's oldest market on the north bank of the Perfume River. The food stalls are on the ground floor; handicrafts and souvenirs are upstairs. Bargain politely, and buy food gifts in sealed packs.

1. Me xung (sesame candy)

Me xung is the souvenir most associated with Hue: roasted sesame seeds, peanuts, malt syrup and sugar pressed into a chewy, lightly sweet candy, sometimes wrapped in rice paper, in chewy and crisp versions. It keeps well, costs very little, and is the gift every Hue family sends to relatives. The well-known local makers are Thien Huong (20 Chi Lang Street) and Nam Thuan (135 Huynh Thuc Khang Street), but the easiest place to buy it is the food section of Dong Ba Market.

  • Price: About 20,000-100,000 VND (~$1-4) per box
  • Where to buy: Dong Ba Market (Place block in Section D)

2. Tom chua (sour shrimp)

Tom chua is Hue's fermented shrimp: small shrimps fermented with garlic, chilli, galangal and sticky rice wine until sour, salty and fragrant. Locals eat it with boiled pork and herbs. It is sold in jars that travel well — but it is a fermented product with a strong smell, so pack it in a sealed bag and check your airline's rules for liquids and food.

  • Price: Not verified — priced by the jar at the stall
  • Where to buy: Dong Ba Market (Place block in Section D); look for the Co Ri stall inside the market

3. Non la bai tho (poetry conical hat)

The poem hat is Hue's most poetic souvenir: a conical hat woven from palm leaves with a poem or a picture of the Perfume River, Truong Tien Bridge or a pagoda hidden between two layers of leaf — invisible until you hold it up to the light. Ordinary conical hats are cheap and everywhere; the poem hat is the Hue version, made in Phu Cam Village (see Section O).

  • Price: About 150,000-500,000 VND (~$6-19) per hat, depending on the complexity of the poem and design
  • Where to buy: Dong Ba Market (Place block in Section D) or directly from the artisans in Phu Cam Village

O. Craft Villages

1. Thuy Xuan Incense Village

Thuy Xuan, about 7 km southwest of the city centre on the way to the royal tombs, has made incense for centuries — it once supplied the Nguyen court. The village street is famous for its bundles of incense sticks in bright colours (yellow, red, green, purple) drying in the sun, and it is one of the most photographed streets in Hue. Walk in, watch the artisans roll, dye and dry the sticks by hand, and buy a bundle directly from a maker. Some shops also rent ao dai for photos (about 50,000-70,000 VND, ~$2-3).

2. Phu Cam Conical Hat Village

Phu Cam, on the southern bank of the An Cuu River in Phuoc Vinh Ward, has made conical hats since the 17th century and invented the poem hat: artisans slip coloured paper with poems or pictures between two layers of palm leaf, so the verse appears only in sunlight. The village is small and quiet — fewer than 50 active artisans — and you can watch the whole process: splitting bamboo for the 16-brim frame, drying and ironing the leaves, and sewing the hat with fine thread. It is a 15-minute walk from the Citadel area.
