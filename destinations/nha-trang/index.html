A. Overview

Nha Trang is Vietnam's classic beach city — a long curve of sand, a palm-lined promenade, and a bay scattered with islands, backed by mountains that turn gold at sunset. It is less hectic than Ho Chi Minh City and more built-up than Phu Quoc, which makes it an easy first beach stop: everything is close together, the food is excellent, and there is enough to do for 3 to 4 days without ever feeling rushed.

Most visitors need 3 to 4 days. One day for the city itself (Po Nagar Cham Towers, Long Son Pagoda, Dam Market), one day for VinWonders on Hon Tre Island, and one day for island snorkeling or a mud bath. Add a fourth day for Ba Ho Waterfalls or a slow beach day. The best weather runs February to August, when the sea is calm and the rain is rare. September to December brings the rainy season, with October and November the wettest — the sea gets rough and boat trips are often cancelled.

Nha Trang suits families (the theme park, the aquarium, calm shallow water), couples (sunset cruises, spa days, beachfront dinners), and first-timers who want a beach holiday with real Vietnamese city life attached — not a sealed resort bubble. Backpackers will find the Biet Thu and Hung Vuong area cheap and cheerful.

Getting here is straightforward. Flights marketed as "Nha Trang" actually land at Cam Ranh International Airport (CXR), about 35 km south of the city — a 40 to 50 minute drive. Trains from Ho Chi Minh City take 7 to 8.5 hours and arrive at Nha Trang Railway Station, a short ride from the beachfront. Sleeper buses from Ho Chi Minh City take 8 to 9 hours.

Prices checked November 2026. Exchange rate used in this guide: 1 USD ≈ 26,000 VND.

B. Getting There

By plane. Cam Ranh International Airport (CXR) has direct flights from Ho Chi Minh City (about 1 hour), Hanoi (about 2 hours), Da Nang, and a growing list of international cities including Seoul, Bangkok, and Singapore. Airlines include Vietnam Airlines, VietJet Air, and Bamboo Airways. From the airport to central Nha Trang: the airport shuttle bus (route 18) costs 50,000–65,000 VND (~$2–$2.50) per person and takes 50 to 60 minutes; a taxi costs 300,000–400,000 VND (~$12–$15) and takes 40 to 50 minutes; Grab is in the same range as a taxi, roughly 250,000–350,000 VND (~$10–$13).

By train. Nha Trang Railway Station sits almost in the city center. Trains from Ho Chi Minh City run 7 times a day; the fastest, SE2, takes 7h09m. Seats start from around 347,000 VND (~$13), soft seats from around 375,000 VND (~$14), and 4-berth sleeper berths from around 591,000 VND (~$23). Overnight sleepers run on 3 of the 7 daily services, so you can leave Saigon in the evening and wake up at the beach. From Hanoi, the journey takes about 25 hours — fly instead.

By bus. Sleeper buses from Ho Chi Minh City take 8 to 9 hours and cost from around 370,000 VND (~$14). Phuong Trang (FUTA Bus) and other operators run overnight services. Buses arrive at the southern or northern bus stations; a Grab ride to Tran Phu beachfront costs around 50,000–80,000 VND (~$2–$3).

By private car. A private car from Ho Chi Minh City takes 6 to 7 hours and costs roughly 3,000,000–4,500,000 VND (~$115–$173) per car. From Da Lat, the mountain road takes about 3.5 to 4 hours by car or bus.

C. Getting Around

On foot. The beachfront strip along Tran Phu Street is flat, walkable, and made for evening strolls. Most central hotels, restaurants, and the night market are within a 15-minute walk of each other.

Grab and taxis. Grab works well in Nha Trang for both cars and motorbikes, with fares shown before you book. For street taxis, Mai Linh (call 1055) is the trusted brand; Vinasun also operates here. A ride across the city center usually costs 40,000–80,000 VND (~$1.50–$3). The airport run is the one fare worth agreeing on in advance.

Motorbike rental. Scooters rent for roughly 100,000–150,000 VND (~$4–$6) per day from hotels and rental shops around Biet Thu Street. The coastal road north to Ba Ho Waterfalls and south to the airport is a lovely ride, but wear a helmet and carry an international driving permit with a motorcycle endorsement.

Bicycle. Several hotels lend bicycles for free, and the flat promenade is ideal for a morning ride. Rental shops near the beach charge around 50,000–100,000 VND (~$2–$4) per day.

Boat. Island trips leave from Cau Da Port, at the southern end of Tran Phu. Book through your hotel or a licensed operator at the pier — not from touts on the beach. A full-day island-hopping and snorkeling trip typically costs 400,000–800,000 VND (~$15–$31) per person including lunch.

D. Things to Do

Nha Trang's bay is the star, but the city has real history too. Start with the Cham towers and the pagoda in the morning, when the light is kind and the heat is still bearable, and save the beach for the afternoon.

E. Food & Drink

Nha Trang's food is all about the sea. The signatures are bun cha ca (fish-cake noodle soup), bun sua (jellyfish noodle soup — the jellyfish is crunchy, not slimy), nem nuong Ninh Hoa (grilled pork rolls wrapped in rice paper with herbs and crispy fried spring rolls), and bo nuong Lac Canh (spicy grilled beef cooked on a small charcoal stove at your table). Seafood restaurants along the northern end of Tran Phu and around the old fishing harbor grill the morning's catch to order.

Vegetarian and allergies: Vegetarian restaurants cluster around the pagodas and on Tran Phu — look for "chay" on the sign. Seafood is the main allergy risk here: shrimp paste and fish sauce are in almost every noodle soup, so tell the staff clearly and show a written allergy card if your allergy is serious. The night market on Tran Phu has plenty of grilled corn, fruit, and vegetarian-friendly snacks if you want to graze.

F. Where to Stay

Stay on or just behind Tran Phu Street for the beach, the promenade, and the restaurants. The northern end (near the Sheraton and InterContinental) is quieter and more upmarket; the middle stretch around Biet Thu and Hung Vuong is the backpacker and mid-range zone, with hostels, bars, and cheap eats; the southern end near Cau Da Port is closest to the boat pier and VinWonders cable car. Dorm beds run roughly 150,000–250,000 VND (~$6–$10) a night, mid-range hotels 600,000–1,500,000 VND (~$23–$58), and beachfront resorts from 2,500,000 VND (~$96) and up.

Book through Agoda (https://www.agoda.com) or Booking.com (https://www.booking.com) — both list Nha Trang's full range, and prices swing a lot by season.

G. Sample Itineraries

1 day — the city. Morning: Po Nagar Cham Towers at opening, then Long Son Pagoda. Lunch: nem nuong at Dang Van Quyen. Afternoon: Dam Market, then the beach and a swim. Evening: seafood dinner on Tran Phu and a walk along the promenade.

2 days — add the island. Day 1 as above. Day 2: VinWonders on Hon Tre Island — take the cable car at opening, do the rides and aquarium in the morning, the water park in the afternoon, and stay for the evening show if your energy holds.

3 to 4 days — slow down. Day 3: island-hopping and snorkeling at Hon Mun in the morning, then a mud bath at I-Resort in the afternoon. Day 4: Ba Ho Waterfalls in the morning (or a cooking class), a long beach afternoon, and a sunset at Hon Chong.

H. Money & Budget

Prices checked November 2026.

  • Backpacker: 600,000–900,000 VND (~$23–$35) per day — dorm bed, street food and market meals, walking and Grab bikes, beach free.
  • Mid-range: 1,200,000–2,200,000 VND (~$46–$85) per day — comfortable hotel, restaurant meals, Grab cars, one paid activity a day.
  • Comfortable: 3,000,000–4,500,000 VND (~$115–$173) per day — beachfront resort, fine dining, private boat trip, spa and mud bath.

Cash is still the default at markets, street stalls, and small restaurants — carry small notes. ATMs line Tran Phu Street and accept foreign cards, usually with a fee of 50,000–100,000 VND per withdrawal. Cards are accepted at hotels, resorts, and mid-range restaurants. Currency exchange shops on Tran Phu and Biet Thu offer better rates than the airport; gold shops in the old town also exchange cash at good rates. Tipping is not expected, but rounding up or leaving 10% at a sit-down restaurant is appreciated.

I. Safety & SOS

Nha Trang is one of Vietnam's safer beach cities, but the usual rules apply. Bag-snatching happens on the promenade at night — wear your bag across your body and keep your phone out of your back pocket. Swim only in marked areas: the bay looks calm, but currents and undertow can be strong after rain, and jellyfish appear in some seasons (ask the lifeguards). On boat trips, insist on a life jacket and check that the boat has one for every passenger.

  • Emergency numbers: Police 113, Fire 114, Ambulance 115.
  • Common scams: Overpriced boat trips sold by beach touts (book through your hotel or at the pier); jet-ski and sun-lounger "damage" charges (agree on the price and photograph the equipment first); taxi meters that run fast (use Grab or Mai Linh 1055). During the rainy season, check the weather before booking any boat trip — operators should cancel in rough seas, but some do not.

Seasonal hazards

Nha Trang is dry from February to August and rainy from September to December. October and November are the wettest months (roughly 280–340 mm each) and carry the typhoon risk: three typhoons made direct landfall here in the last ten years, all between late October and early November. When a storm approaches, the province bans boats from leaving port — in November 2025 more than 300 tourist vessels were halted for Typhoon Kalmaegi, and days of rain flooded low-lying districts, with water over two metres in places and landslides on the Khanh Le and Khanh Son passes. Island trips to Hon Mun run easiest from January to August. See the full picture on our Seasonal Hazards & Natural Dangers page.

J. Local Tips

Nha Trang runs at a slower pace than Saigon, and that is the point. Mornings belong to the locals — fishermen, market shoppers, and tai chi groups on the beach — so set an alarm at least once. Dress modestly at the Cham towers and pagodas (shoulders and knees covered, shoes off inside). Bargaining is expected at Dam Market and with beach vendors, but not in fixed-price shops.

Weather: February to August is dry and sunny (26–33°C), with the calmest sea from March to August. September to December is the rainy season — October and November can bring days of heavy rain and rough seas, and boat trips are often cancelled. Pack sunscreen, a hat, swimwear, and water shoes for the rocky beaches north of the city. A light rain jacket is worth it even in the dry season for the occasional shower.

  • VinWonders Nha Trang tickets: Book through Klook (https://www.klook.com) or GetYourGuide (https://www.getyourguide.com) — online prices are usually the same as the gate, but you skip the ticket queue. Combo tickets with the cable car are the standard option.
  • Island-hopping and snorkeling: Viator (https://www.viator.com) and GetYourGuide list half-day and full-day trips to Hon Mun and the nearby islands, typically 400,000–800,000 VND (~$15–$31) per person including lunch and gear.
  • Mud bath packages: I-Resort and Thap Ba Hot Spring can be booked through Klook, or just walk in — tickets are sold at the door.
  • City and food tours: Klook and GetYourGuide list half-day city tours covering Po Nagar, Long Son, and Dam Market, and evening street-food walks from around 500,000 VND (~$19) per person.

M. Photos & Map

Photo spots: The white Buddha viewpoint at Long Son Pagoda at golden hour; the cable car crossing to Hon Tre at sunset; Hon Chong Promontory at dusk; the fishing boats at Cau Da Port in the early morning; and the Tran Phu promenade at blue hour, when the city lights come on along the bay.

N. Local Specialties & Souvenirs

Nha Trang's best souvenirs are edible, and they all come from the sea or the hills of Khanh Hoa. Shop in the morning, when the markets are liveliest, and check expiry dates on anything packaged.

Khanh Hoa bird's nest (yến sào). The province's most famous gift — and its most expensive. Swiftlets nest in the sea caves of the islands off Khanh Hoa, and the state-run Khanh Hoa Salanganes Nest Company manages the harvest. Dried nests are sold as a health tonic; ready-to-drink bottles make a lighter, cheaper gift. Expect about 2,500,000–3,500,000 VND (~$96–135) per 100 g for farmed house nest, and from about 9,000,000 VND (~$346) per 100 g for wild island nest. Buy from the official showroom, not a market stall — fakes are common.

Dried seafood. Dried squid, shrimp, fish and stingray are the classic Nha Trang take-home: light, packable, and they keep for weeks. Dried squid runs about 500,000–700,000 VND (~$19–27) per kg. Look for mực rim me — dried squid simmered in a sweet-sour tamarind sauce — the snack locals buy by the bag.

Nem chua Ninh Hoa (fermented pork rolls) and mango rice paper. Nem chua from nearby Ninh Hoa town is the region's signature cured pork: small pink rolls wrapped in banana leaf, tangy and garlicky, eaten raw. Mango rice paper (bánh tráng xoài), made from Cam Ranh mangoes, is a chewy, translucent sweet that travels well. Both are sold all over Dam Market.

Sa Pa sits high in the Hoang Lien Son mountains of north-west Vietnam, about 320 km from Hanoi, near the Chinese border.

Phong Nha is a small, sleepy town on the Son River in central Vietnam, and the gateway to Phong Nha-Ke Bang National Park — a UNESCO World Heritage site and one of the great cave landscapes on Earth.
