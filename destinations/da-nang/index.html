A. Overview

Da Nang sits on Vietnam's central coast, where the Han River meets the South China Sea. It is the country's most liveable big city — and one of the easiest places in Vietnam for a first-timer to enjoy. In a single weekend you can ride a cable car to the Golden Bridge in the clouds, climb the Marble Mountains, swim at My Khe Beach, eat your way through the morning markets, and watch the Dragon Bridge breathe fire on a Saturday night.

The city works well as a base: Hoi An is 30 minutes south, Hue is about 2.5 hours north by train over the Hai Van Pass, and the airport is only 10–15 minutes from downtown. Give Da Nang itself 2–3 days. The best weather runs from February to August — dry, sunny and warm. September to December brings rain and the occasional typhoon, so check the forecast if you travel then.

Da Nang suits almost everyone: families (easy transport, beaches, Ba Na Hills), couples (seafood dinners on the beach road, sunset at Son Tra), and first-timers who want Vietnam without the chaos of Hanoi or Ho Chi Minh City. Prices checked November 2026.

B. Getting There

By plane. Da Nang International Airport (DAD) is about 3 km from the city centre, a 10–15 minute drive. Vietnam Airlines, VietJet Air and Bamboo Airways fly from Hanoi and Ho Chi Minh City in about 1.5 hours, several times a day. Fares change with the season, so check prices when you book — there is no fixed fare to quote here.

By train. Da Nang Railway Station is on the main north–south line. The train from Hanoi takes about 16 hours; from Ho Chi Minh City about 17–18 hours. C. Getting Around

Da Nang is flat, compact and easy to get around. Grab works well here for cars and motorbikes, and prices are shown up front, which removes the haggling. For street taxis, use these verified companies:

  • Mai Linh Taxi: 0236 3 56 56 56
  • Vinasun Taxi: 0236 3 68 68 68
  • Tien Sa Taxi: 0236 3 79 79 79

Always ask the driver to run the meter, and check that it starts at the flag-fall rate. A ride across the city centre usually costs 50,000–100,000 VND (~$2–4). A Grab or taxi to Hoi An (about 30 km, 30 minutes) costs 300,000–400,000 VND (~$12–15).

Motorbike rental costs about 150,000–200,000 VND (~$6–8) per day. You legally need an International Driving Permit with a motorbike endorsement to ride in Vietnam, and your travel insurance may not cover you without one. Wear a helmet, keep your speed down, and photograph the bike before you take it — rental shops sometimes claim damage you did not cause.

Walking works well in the riverside and beach areas, but the city is spread out, so you will still want a ride for Ba Na Hills, the Marble Mountains and Son Tra Peninsula.

E. Food & Drink

Da Nang's food is Central Vietnamese: bold, spicy and cheap. Mi Quang — turmeric noodles with pork, shrimp and herbs in a little broth — is the dish to try first. Bun cha ca (fish-cake noodle soup) is the breakfast of choice, and the beach road is lined with seafood restaurants where you pick your catch from the tanks.

Vegetarian and allergies: Vegetarian restaurants exist in the city centre, but most street-food stalls cook with fish sauce and shrimp paste. If you have a peanut or shellfish allergy, learn the phrase "Toi bi di ung" (I am allergic) and name the ingredient — see section L. F. Where to Stay

Stay in one of two areas: along the Han River (Bach Dang Street) for the city, markets and Dragon Bridge, or along My Khe Beach (Vo Nguyen Giap Street) for the sand and the sea breeze. The beach side is quieter and better for families; the river side is better for food and nightlife.

G. Sample Itineraries

1 day — the highlights. Morning: Marble Mountains, then My Khe Beach for a swim. Afternoon: Museum of Cham Sculpture and Han Market. Evening: dinner on the beach road, then the Dragon Bridge fire show at 21:00 (Friday–Sunday).

2 days — add Ba Na Hills. Day 1 as above. Day 2: full day at Ba Na Hills and the Golden Bridge (leave by 07:30, back by late afternoon). Evening: Linh Ung Pagoda at sunset, then a seafood dinner on Vo Nguyen Giap Street.

3 days — add Son Tra and Hoi An. Day 3: morning ride around Son Tra Peninsula, afternoon day trip to Hoi An (30 minutes away) for the lanterns, or take the train to Hue for the day if you prefer history over tailors.

H. Money & Budget

Prices checked November 2026. 1 USD ≈ 26,000 VND.

  • Backpacker: about 700,000–1,000,000 VND (~$27–38) per day — hostel dorm, street food, Grab, one paid attraction.
  • Mid-range: about 1,500,000–2,500,000 VND (~$58–96) per day — comfortable hotel, restaurant meals, taxis, Ba Na Hills or a day trip.
  • Comfortable: about 3,500,000 VND+ (~$135+) per day — beachfront resort, seafood dinners, private car and driver.

ATMs are everywhere in the centre and along the beach road; most accept foreign cards and charge 20,000–50,000 VND (~$0.80–1.90) per withdrawal. Credit cards work in hotels, larger restaurants and malls, but street food, markets and small shops are cash-only. Bring small notes — 500,000 VND bills are hard to break at a noodle stall. Currency exchange is available at banks and gold shops on Hung Vuong Street; avoid the airport exchange counters, whose rates are poor.

Scams to watch for: Taxi drivers who "forget" the meter or take a long route — use Grab or a verified company. Motorbike rental shops that claim damage on return — photograph the bike first. Market vendors who quote a tourist price — bargain politely, and walk away if the price does not move. Seasonal hazards

October is the wettest month (about 628 mm of rain on long-term averages) and November is not far behind (about 448 mm). Typhoon season on the central coast runs from September to December, peaking from early October to mid-November. Heavy rain floods underpasses and riverside roads, and saturated hillsides raise the landslide risk on Son Tra, the Hai Van Pass and the Ba Na Hills road — the city warned visitors off all three during the October 2026 storms. The sea is rough from October to January; swim only where lifeguards are on duty. The best window is February to August. See the full picture on our Seasonal Hazards & Natural Dangers page.

J. Local Tips

Seasons: February to August is dry and sunny, with temperatures of 28–35°C. September to December is the rainy season — pack a rain jacket and expect afternoon downpours. January is cool and pleasant (20–25°C) but can be overcast.

Culture and etiquette: Dress modestly at pagodas and temples — cover shoulders and knees. Remove shoes when entering a temple hall or someone's home. Pointing with feet is rude; so is touching someone's head. A smile and a small bow go a long way.

Bargaining: Expected at markets and with street vendors, not in restaurants, shops with price tags, or taxis with meters. Stay friendly, start at about half, and accept the deal with a smile.

What to pack: Sunscreen (the sun is fierce even on cloudy days), a rain jacket in the wet season, comfortable walking shoes for the Marble Mountains' steep steps, and a light layer for Ba Na Hills — it is 1,400 m up and noticeably cooler than the city.

N. Local Specialties & Souvenirs

Da Nang's edible souvenirs are Central Vietnamese: sesame sweets, beef sausage and dried seafood, all sold in the dry-goods section of Han Market (Place block in Section D). Most stalls vacuum-pack for travel, and the ground floor is where the food is — the upstairs is fabric and clothing. Start bargaining at about half the first price and meet in the middle.

1. Banh kho me (sesame dry cake)

Banh kho me is Da Nang's signature sweet: a crisp, lightly sweet cake of glutinous rice, sugar, peanuts and roasted sesame, from Cam Le on the city's edge. It keeps well, travels well, and is the gift Hanoians and Saigonese ask for from Da Nang. Look for the Ba Lieu brand, the best-known maker.

  • Price: About 30,000-50,000 VND (~$1-2) per pack of 200-300 g
  • Where to buy: Han Market (Place block in Section D)

2. Cha bo (beef sausage)

Cha bo is Da Nang's firm, fragrant beef sausage — lean beef pounded with garlic, pepper and fish sauce, steamed in banana leaves. Locals eat it sliced with banh mi or rice; visitors buy it vacuum-packed as a gift. It is perishable, so buy it on your last day and keep it cool.

  • Price: Not verified — priced by weight at the stall; agree the price before the vendor slices it
  • Where to buy: Han Market (Place block in Section D)

3. Dried seafood

Dried squid, dried shrimp and fish snacks are the other Han Market staple — most stalls vacuum-pack them for the suitcase, and they are the gift that tastes most like the Central coast.

  • Price: Priced by weight; compare two or three stalls before buying
  • Where to buy: Han Market (Place block in Section D)

4. Non Nuoc stone carvings

Small marble bracelets, rings, pendants, incense holders and animal figures, carved in the village at the foot of the Marble Mountains. They are the most durable Da Nang souvenir — nothing to eat, nothing to break. See Section O for the village itself.

O. Craft Villages

1. Non Nuoc Stone Carving Village

Non Nuoc has carved stone since the late 17th century, when artisan Huynh Ba Quat came from Thanh Hoa and settled at the foot of the Marble Mountains. Today the village has around 500 workshops, and in 2014 it was recognised as a National Intangible Cultural Heritage site. Walk the streets around the Marble Mountains and you will hear chisels all day: artisans turning marble, limestone and sandstone into statues, temple pieces, jewellery and souvenirs. There is no entrance fee — just walk in, watch the work, and buy from the workshop that made the piece. Combine it with the Marble Mountains, which are right next door. The Place block, address and hours are in Section N above.

Real vs resin: the village's reputation rests on natural stone, but some cheap souvenir stalls near the Marble Mountains entrance sell resin copies. Real marble feels cold to the touch, is heavy for its size, and has natural veining that never repeats exactly. If a "marble" bracelet is light, warm and perfectly uniform, it is probably resin.

P. Experiences With Locals

1. Cooking class with Jolie — market, farm and home kitchen

Jolie is a local host from Hoi An who runs cooking classes from her family home in Da Nang. The full experience starts in an organic garden (farming life, herbs, picking what you will cook), continues to Han Market to shop for fresh ingredients together, then moves to a home-style kitchen where you cook four Vietnamese dishes hands-on and eat them together — and finishes with a traditional lantern-making session. Shorter versions skip the farm and lantern parts and focus on the market and cooking. Classes are small (up to 9-10 people), vegetarian and vegan menus are available, and there is a public-holiday surcharge. Book at https://joliecookingclass.com — the official site lists the current packages and prices.
