A. Overview

Hoi An is Vietnam's prettiest town — a riverside old port of yellow walls, wooden shophouses and hundreds of silk lanterns that glow every evening. By day it is a slow wander through tailor shops, coffee houses and craft villages; by night it becomes a lantern-lit postcard, with paper lanterns floating down the Hoai River. It is small, flat and made for walking, and it pairs perfectly with Da Nang (30 minutes away) or as a gentle stop on the way to Hue.

A note on the address: on 1 July 2026, Quang Nam province was merged into Da Nang, so Hoi An is now officially part of Da Nang City. Older listings still say "Quang Nam" — both refer to the same place.

Give Hoi An 2–3 days: one for the Ancient Town, one for the countryside and beaches, and a third if you want a slower pace or a tailor-made outfit. The best weather is February to August — dry, warm and sunny. September to December brings rain, and October–November can flood the old town. Hoi An suits couples (it is Vietnam's most romantic town), families (safe, walkable, beaches nearby), food lovers, and anyone who wants to slow down after the big cities. Prices checked November 2026.

B. Getting There

By plane. Hoi An has no airport. Fly into Da Nang International Airport (DAD), about 30 km north — a 30-minute drive. From the airport, a Grab or taxi to Hoi An costs 300,000–400,000 VND (~$12–15). Many hotels arrange airport pick-up for a similar price; ask when you book.

By train. Hoi An has no train station. Take the train to Da Nang Railway Station (791 Hai Phong Street, Da Nang), then a taxi or Grab for the last 30 km (300,000–400,000 VND, ~30 minutes). The train from Hue to Da Nang takes 2.5–3 hours and costs 100,000–250,000 VND (~$4–10) — the coastal stretch over the Hai Van Pass is one of the prettiest rides in Asia.

By bus or car. Sleeper buses from Hanoi and Ho Chi Minh City stop in Da Nang; from there, take a taxi or a local shuttle to Hoi An. From Hue, a private car takes about 3 hours via Da Nang and costs roughly 1,000,000–1,500,000 VND (~$38–58).

C. Getting Around

The Ancient Town is best explored on foot. Cars are banned from the main streets (Tran Phu, Nguyen Thai Hoc, Bach Dang) during pedestrian hours, 09:00–11:00 and 15:00–21:30, so the old town is pleasantly quiet. Bicycles are the classic way to reach the beaches and villages — rentals are available everywhere in town; prices were not verified, so agree the rate before you ride. Grab works in Hoi An for cars and motorbikes, and the Da Nang taxi companies also serve the town:

  • Mai Linh Taxi: 0236 3 56 56 56
  • Vinasun Taxi: 0236 3 68 68 68
  • Tien Sa Taxi: 0236 3 79 79 79

No verified Hoi An-specific taxi number found — use Grab or one of the Da Nang companies above. A Grab to An Bang Beach (about 4 km) costs 50,000–80,000 VND (~$2–3). Motorbike rental costs about 150,000–200,000 VND (~$6–8) per day; you legally need an International Driving Permit with a motorbike endorsement, and a helmet is mandatory.

E. Food & Drink

Hoi An has three dishes you will not find done the same way anywhere else: cao lau (thick noodles with pork and greens, made with water from a specific local well), banh mi (the town's version is famous worldwide), and com ga (chicken rice). Eat them in the Old Town, then walk it off along the river.

Vegetarian and allergies: Hoi An is one of the easiest towns in Vietnam for vegetarians — several dedicated vegetarian restaurants serve cao lau and banh mi without meat, and most restaurants will make a vegetarian version if you ask ("khong thit" — no meat). G. Sample Itineraries

1 day — the old town. Morning: wander the Ancient Town, visit the Japanese Bridge and one or two heritage houses. Lunch: banh mi from Phuong or Madam Khanh. Afternoon: a tailor fitting or a coffee by the river. Evening: dinner at Morning Glory, then a lantern boat on the Hoai River and the night market.

2 days — add the countryside. Day 1 as above. Day 2: morning cycling to Tra Que Vegetable Village for a cooking class, afternoon at An Bang Beach, evening back in town for cao lau and the lanterns.

3 days — add My Son. Day 3: early start to My Son Sanctuary (before 08:15 to beat the crowds), back by lunch, then a slow afternoon — a basket boat in the Bay Mau Coconut Forest, or a beach afternoon and a final lantern-lit dinner.

H. Money & Budget

Prices checked November 2026. 1 USD ≈ 26,000 VND.

  • Backpacker: about 700,000–1,000,000 VND (~$27–38) per day — hostel or budget guesthouse, street food, bicycle, one or two heritage tickets.
  • Mid-range: about 1,500,000–2,500,000 VND (~$58–96) per day — boutique hotel, restaurant meals, Grab, a basket boat or cooking class.
  • Comfortable: about 3,500,000 VND+ (~$135+) per day — riverside resort, private driver, My Son tour, tailor-made clothes.

ATMs are plentiful in the Old Town and along the main streets; most accept foreign cards and charge 20,000–50,000 VND (~$0.80–1.90) per withdrawal. Cards work in hotels and larger restaurants, but street food, markets, tailors and boat rides are cash-only. Bring small notes — a 500,000 VND bill is hard to break at a banh mi stall. Tailors usually take a deposit in cash and the balance on collection; agree the total price in writing before they start cutting.

Scams to watch for: Tailors who pressure you into ordering more than you want — decide your budget before you walk in, and get the price in writing. Boat touts who quote one price and charge another — agree the price and the duration before boarding. The "free" lantern that turns out to cost 50,000 VND — nothing on the riverfront is free. Flooding in October–November can close the old town streets; check conditions with your hotel before you go out.

Seasonal hazards

The Ancient Town floods in most years in October and November, when the Thu Bon River rises after sustained rain. Most floods are shallow and brief — knee-deep water on the lowest riverside streets for one to three days — but exceptional years have seen water approaching two metres (1964, 1999, 2007, 2017, 2020), and in October 2025 the river peaked 14 cm above its 1964 mark. October averages about 628 mm of rain, November about 448 mm. If you visit then, stay a little away from the river, keep your plans flexible, and check conditions with your hotel before you go out. The sea is rough from October to January — swim only where lifeguards are on duty. See the full picture on our Seasonal Hazards & Natural Dangers page.

J. Local Tips

Seasons: February to August is dry and sunny, 28–35°C. September to December is the rainy season; October–November can bring floods that close the old town for a day or two. January is cooler (20–25°C) and pleasant.

Culture and etiquette: Dress modestly at pagodas and temples. Remove shoes when entering a temple hall or someone's home. Bargain with a smile at markets and with tailors, never in restaurants or shops with price tags. The old town is a living neighbourhood, not a theme park — keep noise down in the residential lanes at night.

Tailors: Hoi An's tailors are famous, and a made-to-measure suit or dress in 24–48 hours is a real thing here. Get recommendations from your hotel, compare two shops, agree the fabric and price in writing, and leave time for at least one fitting. A rushed job is a bad job.

What to pack: Sunscreen, a hat, comfortable walking shoes, a rain jacket in the wet season, and clothes you can get wet in for the basket boats. Bring a small daypack for the beach — and leave room in your luggage for lanterns.

N. Local Specialties & Souvenirs

Hoi An is the best shopping town in Vietnam for handmade things — lanterns, silk and pottery made in the town itself, not imported. The rule here is simple: buy from the workshop that made it, and bargain kindly (start at about 50% of the asking price on souvenirs and settle around 30% off).

1. Silk lanterns

The silk lantern is Hoi An's signature buy: a bamboo frame wrapped in silk, with a wire mechanism that lets the whole lantern collapse flat to about 2-3 cm thick, so it slips into a suitcase. Small desk lanterns (about 20 cm) are the most practical gift; large ceiling pieces are beautiful but hard to carry. Real silk feels soft and almost translucent; the cheaper polyester copies feel stiff and fade within a year.

  • Price: Small lanterns about $3-8 (about 80,000-200,000 VND); large pieces about $12-25 (about 300,000-650,000 VND)
  • Where to buy: Hoi An Night Market (Place block in Section D) has the widest choice; the lantern shops along Tran Phu Street are the other good bet

2. Hoi An silk and tailoring

Hoi An is Vietnam's tailoring capital, and silk is the fabric to buy: scarves, fabric by the metre for a tailored ao dai or suit, and ready-made silk clothing. The silk shops on Tran Phu and Le Loi Streets compete hard, which keeps quality high and prices fair — but check the fabric before you commit, and get a written receipt with the agreed price and pickup time for anything tailored.

3. Banh dau xanh (mung bean cake)

Hoi An's mung bean cake is a small, dense square of ground mung bean, sugar and fat, pressed into a mould — somewhere between fudge and shortbread, gently sweet, and once a dish reserved for kings. The meat-filled version is the local favourite for gifts. A box is cheap, light and keeps for days at room temperature.

O. Craft Villages

1. Thanh Ha Pottery Village

Thanh Ha, about 3 km west of the Ancient Town on the Thu Bon River, has made pottery for centuries — the village supplied bricks and tiles for Hoi An's old houses. Today its workshops make terracotta pots, vases, animal whistles and decorative ware, and the pottery museum and Terracotta Park sit alongside the working lanes. The highlight is sitting at a wheel with a local artisan and shaping your own piece: most workshops let you try for a small fee, and some include a small clay whistle souvenir with the ticket. Go in the morning, when the kilns are going and the lanes are full of pots drying in the sun.

P. Experiences With Locals

1. Lantern-making workshop

In about an hour, a local artisan teaches you to build your own 20 cm silk lantern: bending the bamboo frame, stretching the silk, and painting it if you like. You keep the lantern — it folds flat for the suitcase. It is the most Hoi An experience there is, and one of the cheapest: about $8-9 per person including all materials. Classes run by April Adventure Travel meet in the alley at 05 Tran Phu Street (05/2 Tran Phu, opposite Pho Hoi Hotel) in the Old Town; book on GetYourGuide (search "Lantern-Making Workshop in Hoi An Old Town") or walk in and ask. Longer versions (about 2 hours, about $17-18) add a lantern boat ride on the Hoai River at dusk.

2. Farming and cooking at Tra Que Vegetable Village

Tra Que is an organic vegetable village about 3 km north of the Old Town, where local farmers grow the herbs for Hoi An's kitchens using seaweed fertiliser and no chemicals. The tours are genuinely hands-on: you put on a conical hat, till soil, plant and water vegetables, harvest herbs with the farmers, and sometimes try making rice paper — then a local chef teaches you to cook four Vietnamese dishes with what you picked, and you eat them together. Most tours start with a local market visit and a bicycle ride through the rice paddies. Allow 3.5-5 hours depending on the operator; groups are small (up to 12 people). Book through your hotel or a Hoi An tour desk — several operators run the same route at different prices.

Ha Long Bay is the postcard of Vietnam: nearly 2,000 limestone islands rising out of emerald water in the Gulf of Tonkin, about 170 km east of Hanoi.

2-3 days · Best: October to April

Ninh Binh

Ninh Binh sits about 95-100 km south of Hanoi, on the southern edge of the Red River Delta.

2-3 days · Best: October to April

Sa Pa

Sa Pa sits high in the Hoang Lien Son mountains of north-west Vietnam, about 320 km from Hanoi, near the Chinese border.

2-3 days · Best: September to November and March to May

Phong Nha

Phong Nha is a small, sleepy town on the Son River in central Vietnam, and the gateway to Phong Nha-Ke Bang National Park — a UNESCO World Heritage site and one of the great cave landscapes on Earth.

2-3 days · Best: February to August

Hue

Hue is Vietnam's old imperial capital — slower, quieter and more graceful than Hanoi or Ho Chi Minh City.

2-3 days · Best: February to August
