A. Overview

Ha Long Bay is the postcard of Vietnam: nearly 2,000 limestone islands rising out of emerald water in the Gulf of Tonkin, about 170 km east of Hanoi. UNESCO listed it as a World Heritage Site in 1994, and since 2023 the listing covers Ha Long Bay and the Cat Ba Archipelago together — 65,650 hectares and 1,133 islands. The bay is the reason you come. Ha Long City, on the shore, is where you sleep, eat and catch the boat.

The city has two sides, split by the Bai Chay Bridge. Bai Chay, on the west, is the tourist side: the beach, the cruise ports, Sun World, the hotels and the seafood restaurants. Hon Gai, on the east, is the working city: the museum, the local markets, and the ferry to Cat Ba. Most visitors stay in Bai Chay and cross to Hon Gai for an afternoon at the museum.

How many days? A day trip from Hanoi gives you about four hours on the water for twelve hours away from the city — worth it if you are short on time, but tiring. One night in Ha Long City plus a day cruise is a good middle ground. Two days and one night on an overnight cruise is the classic Ha Long experience: the bay goes quiet in the evening, the morning is empty before the day boats arrive, and that is when the place feels like the postcards. Three days lets you add Cat Ba Island or Lan Ha Bay, which is quieter than Ha Long Bay itself.

The best time is October to April. October to December is the sweet spot: clear skies, comfortable temperatures, and the best light. January to March is cooler and mistier — atmospheric, but the bay can disappear into fog for a day at a time. May to September is hot and humid, with the typhoon season running roughly June to September; cruises can be cancelled at short notice when a storm comes in, so keep your schedule flexible in summer and check the forecast before you book a non-refundable cruise.

Ha Long suits first-timers who want the one sight Vietnam is famous for, couples (an overnight cruise is the most romantic night in northern Vietnam), families with children who like boats and caves, and photographers. It suits you less if you want a beach holiday — Bai Chay Beach is pleasant for an afternoon, but Ha Long is a bay for cruising, not a beach resort.

B. Getting There

From Hanoi by road — the only realistic way. The drive is about 160 km and takes 2.5-3 hours on the Hanoi - Hai Phong Expressway (Highway 5B). There is no direct train service between Hanoi and Ha Long Bay — the route has been suspended, and the indirect rail option via Hai Phong takes far longer than a direct road transfer. The old seaplane service was discontinued on 1 April 2026, so road transport is now the only realistic way to make the trip.

A limousine van or cruise shuttle is the best option for most travelers: hotel pickup in Hanoi's Old Quarter, drop-off directly at the cruise harbor (Tuan Chau Marina or Ha Long International Cruise Port), about 2.5-3 hours, for roughly 180,000-350,000 VND (~$7-13) per person one way. Most overnight cruises include the shuttle in the package price — confirm which pier you are dropped at, because Tuan Chau and Bai Chay are not the same place.

Named operators on the Hanoi–Ha Long route include Phuc Xuyen (sleeper bus and limousine from My Dinh, 200,000–260,000 VND / ~$8–10), Kumho Viet Thanh (sleeper bus from My Dinh, about 120,000 VND / ~$5), Ka Long Bus (41-seat sleeper from My Dinh and Giap Bat, 200,000–230,000 VND / ~$8–9), Hung Duc (44-seat sleeper and 16-seat limousine, about 140,000 VND / ~$5) and Ha Long Travel Limousine (limousine, 200,000–240,000 VND / ~$8–9). Check on 12Go/Vexere before booking — fares move with weekends and holidays.

A local public bus from My Dinh, Giap Bat or Gia Lam station in Hanoi costs about 100,000-250,000 VND (~$4-10) and takes 3-4 hours with multiple stops, arriving at Bai Chay or Ha Long bus station — a taxi ride away from the cruise piers. A private car or taxi from Hanoi costs about 1,500,000-2,200,000 VND (~$58-85) one way, and 2,500,000-4,500,000 VND (~$96-173) round trip.

By plane. Van Don International Airport (VDO) is the closest airport, about 50 km from Ha Long City, roughly an hour by road. It has limited flights, mostly domestic plus a few regional routes — for most international visitors, flying into Hanoi and driving is simpler. Cat Bi International Airport in Hai Phong is about 43 km from Ha Long City and has more flights.

C. Getting Around

Ha Long City is spread out, so you will need wheels. Grab and Xanh SM work in Ha Long City, and the fare shows in the app before you book. A short ride inside Bai Chay costs roughly 30,000-70,000 VND (~$1-3). Metered taxis: Mai Linh Quang Ninh 0203 3628 628 and Ha Long Taxi 0203 3626 262 (both checked against two sources). Ask the driver to turn on the meter or agree a fixed price first.

Motorbike rental costs about 100,000-150,000 VND (~$4-6) per day and is a good way to explore Bai Chay, Hon Gai and the coastal road — the roads here are wider and calmer than in Hanoi. You need a valid International Driving Permit plus your home licence, and a helmet is mandatory.

The bay itself is only reachable by boat. Day cruises and overnight cruises leave from Tuan Chau Marina or Ha Long International Cruise Port (confirm which one with your operator — they are in different places). The cable car and a taxi or Grab connect Bai Chay with Hon Gai across the bay; walking across the Bai Chay Bridge is possible but long and exposed to traffic.

D. Things to Do

The bay: how a cruise works

Almost every cruise follows the same shape: board around noon, sail past the famous islets (including the Kissing Rocks and the Fighting Cocks), visit Sung Sot Cave, climb Ti Top Island, kayak or take a bamboo boat near Luon Cave, then anchor for the night or sail back. The difference between cruises is the boat, the food and the group size — not the route.

Two costs are often blurred together, so keep them separate. The government sightseeing fee is set by the Quang Ninh provincial council and published by the bay's management board: 250,000 VND (~$10) per person for the main day routes (VHL1 / VHL2 / VHL5), 200,000 VND (~$8) for the quieter day routes (VHL3 / VHL4), 550,000 VND (~$21) for a route 2 overnight, and 750,000 VND (~$29) for two nights. Almost every operator includes the fee in the headline price — ask anyway, because a small number of budget boats add it at the pier.

The cruise itself is where the real money goes. Day trips from Hanoi run roughly $35-60 (~900,000-1,560,000 VND) at the budget end, $60-100 (~1,560,000-2,600,000 VND) for smaller groups and better food, and $100-150 (~2,600,000-3,900,000 VND) for premium boats. Overnight cruises start around $90-150 (~2,340,000-3,900,000 VND) per person for budget boats, sit at $145-200 (~3,770,000-5,200,000 VND) for the mid-range boats most travelers book, and climb past $250 (~6,500,000 VND) for luxury. With a spare night, go overnight — the quiet evening and the empty morning are worth it.

1. Sung Sot Cave (Surprise Cave) — the bay's biggest cave

The largest and most beautiful cave in Ha Long Bay, on Bo Hon Island about 14 km from Bai Chay. About 200 steps under the forest canopy lead to the entrance, and inside are two huge chambers full of stalactites and stalagmites. It is the highlight of almost every cruise and the busiest stop in the bay — go with the flow of your cruise group and give yourself time at the top for the view back over the bay.

2. Ti Top Island — the best view in the bay

A small island with a crescent beach and a staircase of about 400 steps to the top, where the whole bay opens up below you. Most cruises stop here for swimming and the climb. The beach is small and gets crowded when several boats arrive at once — climb first, swim after.

3. Luon Cave — kayak through the tunnel

Luon Cave is a tunnel cave on Bo Hon Island that can only be entered by kayak or bamboo boat. Passing through the tunnel opens into a calm, enclosed lagoon ringed by cliffs. Kayak rental is sometimes included in the cruise price and sometimes extra — check before you board. If you do not kayak, you can relax on board while others go.

4. Thien Cung Cave (Heavenly Palace Cave) — the quieter cave

Thien Cung Cave sits on Dau Go Island, near the shore, and is the stop on the shorter and quieter cruise routes (VHL3 / VHL4). It is smaller than Sung Sot but less crowded, with colorful lighting on its stalactites. Some day cruises visit Thien Cung instead of Sung Sot — check your route before you book if the cave matters to you.

5. Bai Chay Beach — an afternoon on land

Bai Chay Beach is a long, man-made sandy beach along Ha Long Street, right in front of the hotels. It is pleasant for a late-afternoon swim or a walk at sunset, but it is not the reason to come to Ha Long — the bay is. The beach is free, with sun loungers and umbrellas for rent from nearby vendors.

6. Quang Ninh Museum — the black glass box on the bay

The museum sits on the Hon Gai waterfront, a huge black glass building that reflects the sea and sky. Inside are exhibits on the province's history, culture and coal mining industry — coal is the reason Hon Gai exists. It is a good rainy-day stop and a good reason to cross to the Hon Gai side.

7. Sun World Ha Long and the Queen Cable Car — the view from above

Sun World Ha Long is the big entertainment complex spanning Bai Chay and Ba Deo Hill, connected by the Queen Cable Car — Asia's first reversible cable car system with two-story cabins, listed by Guinness World Records, crossing about 2.17 km over Cua Luc Bay. From the top, the Sun Wheel and the hilltop attractions look back over the city side of the bay. The cable car ticket costs about 350,000-380,000 VND (~$13-15) for adults (sources differ — check the official schedule before you go), children 1-1.4 m about 250,000-280,000 VND, under 1 m free. Hours are roughly 14:00-20:00 Monday to Friday and 09:00-20:00 Saturday and Sunday, but they change with the season and maintenance — the cable car was closed for scheduled maintenance from 28 September to 2 October 2026 and reopened on 10 October 2026, so recheck before a special trip. Note: Dragon Park suspended operations from 10 June 2026 for restructuring, with the last operating day 9 June 2026.

8. Bai Tho Mountain — the city viewpoint

Bai Tho (Poem) Mountain rises over Hon Gai, with a trail to the top and a view across the bay and the city. It is the local's viewpoint, less visited than Ti Top. The trail has been closed at times for safety and maintenance — check locally before you go, and do not climb in wet weather or without proper shoes.

Hidden gems and quieter water

Cua Van Floating Village is a fishing village on the bay, visited on the quieter cruise routes — a look at life on the water that the big cave routes skip. Lan Ha Bay, off Cat Ba Island, is the quieter neighbor to Ha Long Bay: fewer boats, similar karsts, and good kayaking. The Lan Ha Bay day sightseeing fee is around 120,000 VND (~$5), paid at Ben Beo harbor on Cat Ba. If Ha Long Bay feels too busy, Lan Ha Bay is the answer — most travelers reach it from Cat Ba Island, about an hour by ferry from Ha Long City.

E. Food & Drink

Ha Long is a seafood city, and the food is the best reason to spend an evening on land. The local specialties are cha muc (grilled squid patties — chewy, smoky, and the thing to bring home as a gift), fresh steamed shrimp and crab, grilled scallops, and ca nhong (Ha Long grouper). Seafood is priced by weight at most restaurants, so always ask the price per kilogram before you order — a "market price" surprise at the bill is the classic Ha Long complaint.

Hong Hanh is the most famous seafood name in Ha Long, with several branches; the Bai Chay branch at 50 Ha Long Street is the one most visitors go to. Expect about 200,000-400,000 VND (~$8-15) per person. Linh Dan and Thuy Chung on the Bai Chay strip are solid alternatives. For something cheaper, the night market and the street stalls near Bai Chay Beach sell grilled seafood, noodles and beer for a fraction of restaurant prices.

Vegetarian food: the Bai Chay area has a few vegetarian-friendly restaurants, and most seafood restaurants will cook vegetable dishes if you ask — say "Toi an chay" (I eat vegetarian) and "khong nuoc mam" (no fish sauce). Allergies: shellfish is everywhere here; if you have a severe allergy, carry an allergy card written in Vietnamese and bring your own medicine. Drinking water: bottled water everywhere; ice at established restaurants is factory ice and generally safe.

F. Where to Stay

Stay in Bai Chay if you are here for the cruise: the ports, the beach, Sun World and the restaurants are all there. Stay on Reu Island if you want a resort escape (Vinpearl runs a speedboat from Bai Chay Wharf). Stay in Hon Gai if you want the local city and lower prices, and do not mind a taxi to the ports.

Budget travelers will find hostels and small hotels in Bai Chay for roughly 250,000-500,000 VND (~$10-19) per night. Mid-range hotels run about 800,000-2,000,000 VND (~$31-77). High-end resorts run from about 2,400,000 VND (~$92) per night and up — Vinpearl Resort & Spa Ha Long lists rooms from about 3,200,000 VND (~$123) per night. Prices move a lot with weekends, holidays and the cruise season — check again when you book. Compare stays on the official homepages of Agoda and Booking.com (plain links, no affiliate code).

G. Sample Itineraries

1 day — day cruise from Hanoi

08:00 Leave Hanoi by limousine van. 11:30-12:00 Board at Tuan Chau Marina. 12:00-16:30 Cruise: Sung Sot Cave, Ti Top Island, kayaking at Luon Cave, lunch on board. 17:00-18:00 Sail back to port. 20:00 Back in Hanoi. A long day — about four hours on the water for twelve hours away — but it works if you are short on time.

2 days, 1 night — the classic overnight cruise

Day 1: morning van from Hanoi, board at noon, afternoon at Sung Sot Cave and Ti Top Island, kayaking at Luon Cave, sunset on the sundeck, dinner on board, and a quiet night anchored in the bay. Day 2: sunrise and tai chi on deck, a morning visit to a floating village or a second cave, brunch on board, back at port around 11:00-12:00, van back to Hanoi by the evening.

3 days, 2 nights — bay plus city

Days 1-2: as the overnight cruise above. Day 3: a slow morning in Ha Long City — Bai Chay Beach, the Queen Cable Car at sunset, Quang Ninh Museum on the Hon Gai side, and a seafood dinner at Hong Hanh. Or swap day 3 for Cat Ba Island and Lan Ha Bay, the quieter water next door.

H. Money & Budget

Prices checked November 2026.

Daily budgets per person (not including international flights or the Hanoi transfer):

  • Backpacker: 800,000-1,500,000 VND (~$31-58) per day. Small hotel in Bai Chay, street food and the night market, a budget day cruise shared with a group.
  • Mid-range: 2,000,000-4,000,000 VND (~$77-154) per day. Mid-range hotel, seafood dinner, a good day cruise or a budget overnight cruise.
  • Comfortable: from 5,000,000 VND (~$192) per day. High-end resort or a mid-range overnight cruise, private car, seafood at Hong Hanh.

The sightseeing fee is usually included in the cruise price — confirm before you pay, because a small number of budget boats add the 250,000 VND fee at the pier. Cash is useful for the night market, small restaurants and tips on the boat (a tip of 100,000-200,000 VND per person for the crew is customary on overnight cruises, and entirely voluntary). Cards are accepted at hotels, larger restaurants and Sun World. ATMs are in Bai Chay and Hon Gai. The reference rate used in this guide is 1 USD ≈ 26,000 VND.

Ha Long's own scams — and how to handle them

The sightseeing fee double-charge: a budget operator quotes a low cruise price, then adds the 250,000 VND government fee at the pier. Handle it: ask in writing before you book whether the sightseeing fee is included.

Seafood priced by weight: the menu says "market price" and the bill is a shock. Handle it: ask the price per kilogram and confirm the total weight before the kitchen starts cooking.

The too-cheap cruise: a day cruise advertised far below the going rate usually means a crowded boat, a rushed route, or hidden extras. Handle it: compare with the price ranges in section D, read recent reviews, and book through a platform with a clear cancellation policy.

Safety on the water: wear the life jacket when kayaking or on a small boat, and do not swim outside the marked areas. In typhoon season (roughly June to September), cruises can be cancelled at short notice — that is the system working, not a scam, and you should not pressure an operator to sail in bad weather. Keep your phone and camera in a waterproof bag on the boat.

Seasonal hazards

Typhoon season is the big one here: storms reach the Gulf of Tonkin from late May to early November, most often from July to October. When a storm is forecast, Quang Ninh's port authority stops issuing departure permits and every cruise stays in harbour — usually for one to three days (six stoppages between June and October 2025). Decisions usually come 24 to 48 hours before departure, so keep a spare day in your plan and book a cruise with free cancellation. February and March bring the worst visibility of the year — persistent fog and haze, sometimes under 100 metres — and fog can delay or suspend departures too. J. Local Tips

Book the cruise before you book the hotel: the cruise is the trip, and the best boats sell out on weekends. Confirm in writing which pier you depart from (Tuan Chau Marina or Ha Long International Cruise Port), whether the sightseeing fee is included, and what time boarding starts.

Weather by season: October to December is the best — clear and comfortable. January to March is misty and atmospheric, but the bay can vanish into fog. June to September is typhoon season — check the forecast and keep your plans flexible. The bay is most beautiful in the early morning, before the day boats arrive, which is another reason to sleep on the water.

What to pack: a light jacket (it gets cool on deck in the evening, even in summer), sun protection, a waterproof bag for your phone and camera, motion-sickness medicine if you are prone to it (the bay is usually calm, but the open water on the way out can roll), and cash in small notes for tips and the night market.

Bargaining: it applies at the night market and souvenir stalls — bargain gently and be ready to walk away. Do not bargain over cruise prices at the pier; book in advance through a platform instead.

K. Tours & Tickets

For Ha Long, the cruise is the tour. Book a day cruise or an overnight cruise through a platform with reviews and a clear cancellation policy — especially in typhoon season, when a good cancellation policy is worth more than a small discount. Day cruises from Hanoi usually include the limousine transfer; overnight cruises usually include the transfer, meals, kayaking and the sightseeing fee, but check each item.

Book tours and tickets on the official homepages of Klook, GetYourGuide and Viator (plain links, no affiliate code). If you are already in Ha Long City, you can also book a day cruise at the Tuan Chau Marina ticket counters — but the popular boats sell out, so book a day ahead in peak season.

N. Local Specialties & Souvenirs

Ha Long's souvenirs come from the sea — and from the coal mines, which is something you will not find anywhere else in Vietnam. Most visitors shop at Ha Long I Market in Hon Gai (the local side of the city) for food gifts, and at the Bai Chay Night Market for keepsakes. Ask for vacuum packing for anything seafood; without it, it will not survive the flight home.

1. Cha muc (squid sausage)

Cha muc is Ha Long's signature food: fresh squid pounded by hand into a paste, seasoned, shaped into patties and fried until golden and springy. It is the dish Ha Long is proudest of, and the vacuum-packed version is the single best edible souvenir in the city. Hien Nhung is one of the oldest and best-known cha muc makers at Ha Long I Market — look for the hand-pounded, slightly irregular patties, which are the sign of the real thing.

Cha Muc Hien Nhung (Ha Long I Market) market

Address
Kiosks 228-230, Ha Long I Market, Van Xuan Street, Bach Dang Ward, Ha Long City, Quang Ninh Province
Price
About 410,000-450,000 VND (~$16-17) per kg; typical cha muc in the city runs 350,000-500,000 VND (~$13-19) per kg
Hours
Not verified — during Ha Long I Market hours, about 06:00-18:30 daily

Tip: Buy it fried fresh and eat some on the spot with steamed rice rolls (banh cuon cha muc) — that is the local breakfast. For the suitcase, buy the vacuum-packed version and keep it cool.

2. Dried seafood

Dried squid, dried shrimp, fish floss and dried sipunculus (sa sung, the prized "sea worm") are the classic Ha Long food gifts: light, long-lasting and unmistakably from the bay. The dried-goods stalls at Ha Long I Market vacuum-pack everything for travel.

Ha Long I Market (Cho Ha Long I) market

Address
Van Xuan Street, Bach Dang Ward, Ha Long City, Quang Ninh Province
Price
Free to enter; dried seafood priced by weight — agree the price and check the weight before paying
Hours
About 06:00-18:30 daily (some sources list much wider hours for the seafood section)

Tip: The ground floor has handicrafts and souvenirs; the seafood is in the outdoor section. Compare two or three stalls before buying — quality and price vary a lot.

Open in Google Maps

3. Coal carvings

Quang Ninh is Vietnam's coal country, and Ha Long artisans carve the glossy black coal into small statues, animals and ornaments. They are cheap, unbreakable in a suitcase, and genuinely local — no other Vietnamese city sells them. Find them at Ha Long Night Market (Place block in Section D), from about 20,000 VND (~$1) for a small piece.
