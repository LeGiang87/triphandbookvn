A. Overview

Phu Quoc is Vietnam's largest island — a tropical escape in the Gulf of Thailand, closer to Cambodia than to the Vietnamese mainland, with white-sand beaches, fishing villages, jungle interior, and some of the best sunsets in the country. It is the only place in Vietnam where most foreign visitors can stay visa-free for up to 30 days (arriving by air or sea directly to the island, without passing through the mainland — check the current rules for your passport before you book).

Most visitors need 3 to 5 days. Three days covers the highlights: one day for the south (Sao Beach, the Hon Thom cable car, Sunset Town), one day for the north (Vinpearl Safari and VinWonders, Starfish Beach), and one day for Duong Dong, the night market, and a snorkeling trip. Five days lets you slow down properly, which is really the point of an island. The best time to visit is the dry season, November to April, when the sea is calm and the skies are clear. May to October is the rainy season — ferries get cancelled, the sea turns rough, and the island's west-coast beaches lose their calm.

Phu Quoc suits couples and honeymooners (sunset beaches, resort stays), families (the safari, the theme park, calm shallow water at Sao Beach), and anyone who wants a beach holiday with a Vietnamese flavor rather than a generic resort island. It is less of a backpacker scene than it used to be — the island has gone upmarket in recent years — but budget guesthouses still exist around Duong Dong.

Getting here is easy by air: Phu Quoc International Airport (PQC) has direct flights from Ho Chi Minh City (about 1 hour), Hanoi (about 2 hours), and a growing list of international cities. The slower, cheaper route is a bus from Ho Chi Minh City to Ha Tien or Rach Gia, then a high-speed ferry across. Note: Phu Quoc was formerly part of Kien Giang Province; following Vietnam's 2025 administrative changes it is now part of An Giang Province.

B. Getting There

By plane. Phu Quoc International Airport (PQC) is about 10 km from Duong Dong town, a 15 to 20 minute drive. Direct flights from Ho Chi Minh City take about 1 hour and run 20-plus times a day with Vietnam Airlines, VietJet Air, Bamboo Airways, and Sun PhuQuoc Airways; one-way fares booked a few weeks ahead typically run 700,000–1,300,000 VND (~$27–$50), rising to $80–$120 or more in peak season (December–January and Tet). From the airport to Duong Dong, a Grab car costs around 180,000–220,000 VND (~$7–$9); a metered taxi costs roughly 150,000–300,000 VND (~$6–$12); VinBus also serves the airport–Duong Dong–Grand World corridor.

By ferry from Ha Tien. The shorter sea crossing: about 1 to 1.5 hours, with tickets around 185,000–300,000 VND (~$7–$12) per adult. Superdong is the main operator, with several sailings a day. Ferries land at Bai Vong Port on the island's east coast, about 30 minutes by Grab from Duong Dong.

By ferry from Rach Gia. The longer crossing: about 2.5 to 3 hours, with tickets around 290,000–400,000 VND (~$11–$15) per adult. Operators include Superdong, Phu Quoc Express, and Hoa Binh Ship. Rach Gia is a 6 to 7 hour bus ride from Ho Chi Minh City, so the full overland journey takes 10 to 12 hours.

By bus + ferry from Ho Chi Minh City. Sleeper buses run overnight to Ha Tien (8 to 8.5 hours, 150,000–300,000 VND / ~$6–$12) or Rach Gia (6 to 7 hours), then the ferry across. Total cost is roughly 350,000–700,000 VND (~$13–$27) per person, and total time 9 to 12 hours. It only makes sense if you want the Mekong Delta countryside as part of the trip — otherwise, fly.

Getting around the island. VinBus electric buses run routes 17, 19 and 20 across the island, including the airport–Duong Dong–Grand World corridor. Since 1 January 2026 they charge 20,000 VND (~$0.80) for a short hop and 50,000 VND (~$2) for the full route — free if you hold a VinWonders, Safari or Grand World ticket, or are staying at a Vinpearl hotel. The Sun World shuttle between Duong Dong and the Hon Thom cable car / Sunset Town is free. Grab and Xanh SM run on the island at about 80,000–120,000 VND (~$3–5) per ride.

Weather warning: Ferries are weather-dependent. In the rainy season (May–October) rough seas frequently delay or cancel sailings, so always have a backup plan — and a flexible flight — if you choose the ferry route in those months.

C. Getting Around

There is no proper public transport on Phu Quoc. Taxis, Grab, hotel shuttles, and rented scooters do the work. The island is about 50 km long, so distances add up — Duong Dong to Sao Beach is 25 km, to Grand World in the north about 30 km.

Grab and taxis. Grab works in Duong Dong and the main tourist areas, with fares shown before you book — a car from the airport to Duong Dong runs about 180,000–220,000 VND (~$7–$9). Green SM (Xanh SM) electric taxis also cover the island. For street taxis, Mai Linh and Vinasun are the trusted brands. Agree on the meter or a fixed price before you set off, especially for longer runs to the north or south of the island.

Motorbike rental. Scooters rent for roughly 120,000–250,000 VND (~$5–$10) per day from hotels and rental shops in Duong Dong. The island's coastal roads are quieter than the mainland's, which makes Phu Quoc one of the better places in Vietnam to ride — but still wear a helmet, carry an international driving permit with a motorcycle endorsement, and photograph the bike before you take it.

VinBus. Free electric shuttle buses run the airport–Duong Dong–Grand World corridor (route 17, roughly every 15–20 minutes), which is handy if you are staying in the north of the island.

Private car with driver. A car and driver for a full day costs roughly 800,000–1,500,000 VND (~$31–$58), depending on distance. Your hotel can arrange one — it is the most comfortable way to see the south of the island in a day, and the cost splits well between three or four people.

D. Things to Do

Phu Quoc divides neatly into three zones: the south (Sao Beach, the cable car, Sunset Town), the center (Duong Dong town, the night market, Long Beach), and the north (Vinpearl Safari, VinWonders, Grand World, Starfish Beach). Plan one day per zone and you will not feel rushed.

E. Food & Drink

Phu Quoc's food is fishing-village cooking: grilled seafood, fish sauce (the island's nuoc mam is famous across Vietnam), and goi ca trich — raw herring salad with shredded coconut, herbs, and rice paper, the dish to try if you try one. Bun quay (a self-mixed noodle soup you stir yourself at the table) is the local breakfast. Seafood restaurants line Tran Hung Dao in Duong Dong and the night market; for the freshest catch, eat at the floating restaurants and beach shacks in Ham Ninh fishing village on the east coast.

Vegetarian and allergies: Vegetarian options are thinner here than on the mainland — the night market has grilled corn, fruit, and vegetarian noodles, and most restaurants will make a vegetable hotpot if you ask. Shellfish and fish sauce are in almost everything, so show a written allergy card if your allergy is serious. Phu Quoc's fish sauce is famously strong; even the dipping sauce at a seafood restaurant will contain it.

F. Where to Stay

Where you stay on Phu Quoc decides your holiday. Duong Dong / Long Beach is the best base for a first visit: restaurants, the night market, and the airport are all close, and you can reach anywhere on the island from here. An Thoi / Sunset Town in the south suits the cable car, Sao Beach, and resort stays. Ganh Dau / Bai Dai in the north suits families doing Vinpearl Safari and VinWonders, with big resorts and Grand World next door. Ong Lang is the quiet middle — boutique resorts on a pretty beach, good for couples.

Guesthouses around Duong Dong run roughly 400,000–800,000 VND (~$15–$31) a night; mid-range resorts 1,200,000–2,500,000 VND (~$46–$96); luxury resorts from 4,000,000 VND (~$154) and up. Book through Agoda (https://www.agoda.com) or Booking.com (https://www.booking.com) — both list the island's full range, and prices swing a lot by season.

G. Sample Itineraries

1 day — the south. Morning: Hon Thom cable car at opening (9:00), water park and beach on Hon Thom Island. Afternoon: Sao Beach for a swim, then Phu Quoc Prison on the way back north. Evening: sunset at Kiss Bridge in Sunset Town, dinner in An Thoi.

2 days — add the center. Day 1 as above. Day 2: slow morning on Long Beach, lunch in Duong Dong, Ho Quoc Pagoda in the afternoon, Dinh Cau Night Market in the evening.

3 to 5 days — the full island. Day 3: north — Vinpearl Safari in the morning, VinWonders in the afternoon, Grand World after dark. Day 4: snorkeling trip to the An Thoi islands (book through your hotel, typically 500,000–900,000 VND / ~$19–$35 per person including lunch), or Starfish Beach at Rach Vem. Day 5: a slow beach day, a spa afternoon, and a final sunset dinner on Long Beach.

H. Money & Budget

Prices checked November 2026.

  • Backpacker: 800,000–1,200,000 VND (~$31–$46) per day — guesthouse, street food and market meals, rented scooter, beach free.
  • Mid-range: 1,800,000–3,000,000 VND (~$69–$115) per day — comfortable resort, restaurant meals, Grab and a private driver for a day trip.
  • Comfortable: 4,000,000–6,000,000 VND (~$154–$231) per day — luxury resort, fine dining, private boat trip, spa.

Phu Quoc is pricier than the mainland — transport costs more because everything is spread out, and resort restaurants charge resort prices. Cash is still the default at markets, street stalls, and small restaurants — carry small notes. ATMs are in Duong Dong and at the airport; they accept foreign cards, usually with a fee of 50,000–100,000 VND per withdrawal. Cards are accepted at hotels, resorts, and mid-range restaurants. There are currency exchange counters at the airport and on Tran Hung Dao; rates are better than at hotels. Tipping is not expected, but rounding up or leaving 10% at a sit-down restaurant is appreciated.

I. Safety & SOS

Phu Quoc is generally safe, and the main risks are natural ones. Swim only where the water is calm and lifeguards are present — the west coast can have strong currents and big waves in the rainy season, and jellyfish appear in some months. On boat and snorkeling trips, insist on a life jacket and check that the boat has one for every passenger. Scooter accidents are the most common injury on the island: the roads are quieter than the mainland's, but they are also darker at night, and stray dogs are common. Do not ride after dark if you can avoid it.

  • Emergency numbers: Police 113, Fire 114, Ambulance 115.
  • Common scams: Overpriced boat trips sold by touts (book through your hotel); jet-ski and sun-lounger "damage" charges (agree on the price and photograph the equipment first); pearl shops selling fakes at real prices (buy only from reputable, established shops); taxi drivers refusing the meter on long runs (use Grab or agree a fixed price first). In the rainy season, check the weather and the ferry status before any boat trip — operators should cancel in rough seas, but some do not.

Seasonal hazards

From May to October the south-west monsoon brings heavy rain and rough seas on the west coast. Island-hopping and snorkelling tours — and the ferries to the mainland — are cancelled when the sea is too rough, most often from June to September. Do not put a ferry crossing on the day before an international flight. The east and south coasts (Bai Sao, Bai Khem) are more sheltered in those months. In July 2026 a tourist speedboat capsized in rough seas off Hon May Rut and 15 passengers died — book with an established operator, wear the life jacket, and do not go out in rough weather. The dry season, November to April, brings calm seas. See the full picture on our Seasonal Hazards & Natural Dangers page.

J. Local Tips

Phu Quoc moves slowly, and that is the point. The island's fishing villages — Ham Ninh on the east coast, Rach Vem in the north — are still real working places, not theme parks: ask before photographing people, and buy something if you stop at a stall. Dress modestly at pagodas (shoulders and knees covered, shoes off inside the main hall).

Weather: November to April is dry and sunny (25–32°C), with the calmest sea from December to March. May to October is the rainy season — heavy afternoon showers, rough seas, cancelled ferries, and murky water for snorkeling. Pack sunscreen, a hat, swimwear, water shoes for rocky beaches, and a light rain jacket even in the dry season. Mosquito repellent matters at dusk, especially near the jungle interior.

One more practical note: Phu Quoc's 30-day visa exemption applies to most foreign visitors arriving directly to the island — but the rules change, and they do not apply if you arrive via the mainland. Check the current conditions for your passport before you book.

K. Tours & Tickets

  • Hon Thom cable car and VinWonders / Safari tickets: Book through Klook (https://www.klook.com) or GetYourGuide (https://www.getyourguide.com) — online prices are usually the same as the gate, but you skip the ticket queue, and combo tickets are often cheaper online.
  • Island snorkeling trips: Viator (https://www.viator.com) and GetYourGuide list half-day and full-day snorkeling trips to the An Thoi islands, typically 500,000–900,000 VND (~$19–$35) per person including lunch and gear.
  • VinWonders and Safari combo: The one-day combo ticket (1,500,000 VND / ~$58 per adult) is sold on Klook and at the gate, and saves 500,000 VND (~$19) versus buying the two parks separately.
  • Sunset Town and Kiss of the Sea show: Tickets are sold through Klook and at Sunset Town; book ahead in peak season, as the show sells out.

M. Photos & Map

Photo spots: Sao Beach in the early morning, before the day-trippers arrive; the Hon Thom cable car crossing at mid-morning, with the An Thoi archipelago below; the sun setting through the gap in the Kiss Bridge; the fishing boats at Ham Ninh village at dawn; and Long Beach at golden hour, when the whole west coast turns orange.

N. Local Specialties & Souvenirs

Phu Quoc is the rare Vietnamese destination where the souvenirs are things the island actually makes: fish sauce, pepper, sim wine and dried seafood. Buy them where they are produced and you get the real thing at local prices.

Fish sauce (nước mắm Phú Quốc). The island's most famous product, made from fresh anchovies fermented 12–15 months in wooden barrels. Phu Quoc fish sauce holds a Protected Geographical Indication in Vietnam and was the first Vietnamese product to earn PDO status in the EU (2012). A 250 ml bottle costs about 60,000–100,000 VND (~$2–4). Buy at a factory barrel house — Khai Hoan is the oldest and most visitor-friendly — where you can taste different strengths before choosing. One warning: fish sauce cannot go in cabin luggage, so pack bottles in checked bags (and double-bag them).

Phu Quoc pepper. Black, red and white peppercorns from the island's pepper farms, famous for a stronger aroma and cleaner heat than mainland pepper. Black pepper costs about 200,000 VND (~$8) per kg, white pepper about 260,000 VND (~$10) per kg. The farms welcome visitors free of charge — walk the rows, taste peppercorns off the vine, and buy from the family that grew them.

Sim wine (rượu sim). A sweet, ruby-red wine made from wild rose myrtle berries that grow on the island. It is light, fruity and very drinkable, and the syrup version makes a good mixer. Bottles cost about 80,000–200,000 VND (~$3–8).

Dried seafood. Dried squid, anchovies and shrimp from the morning catch, sold all over Duong Dong Market. Vacuum-packing is available at many stalls — ask, and your bag will survive the flight home.

P. Experiences With Locals

Night squid fishing with local fishermen. The island's most hands-on evening: join a local fishing boat at sunset, cruise out into the Gulf of Thailand, and try night squid fishing the way island fishermen do it — dropping a line with a lure and waiting for the tug. If you catch anything, the crew cooks it on board for dinner. Trips run about 4 hours, with pickup from hotels in central Duong Dong (John's Tours is the best-known operator; book at their office in Duong Dong or through GetYourGuide). Go with modest expectations — many nights nobody catches much, and the sunset and the boat ride are half the fun. Bring a light jacket: it gets cool and windy after dark. In the rainy season (May–November) boats may depart from An Thoi or Sao Beach instead, with no sunset view.

A fishing day with a family at Rach Vem. A slower, more personal version: spend a morning or afternoon with a multi-generational fishing family at the floating village of Rach Vem in the far north. You cast nets and fish with traditional techniques, snorkel and forage for scallops and sea urchins in the calm water, and finish with a seafood barbecue at the family's floating house. Bookable as a private experience through Viator — book well ahead, as it is popular and places are limited. Only your group takes part, so it suits families and couples who want the island without the crowds.

Hanoi is Vietnam's capital, and it feels like it — a thousand years old, a little chaotic, and completely itself.

Ha Long Bay is the postcard of Vietnam: nearly 2,000 limestone islands rising out of emerald water in the Gulf of Tonkin, about 170 km east of Hanoi.

2-3 days · Best: October to April

Ninh Binh sits about 95-100 km south of Hanoi, on the southern edge of the Red River Delta.

2-3 days · Best: October to April

Sa Pa

Sa Pa sits high in the Hoang Lien Son mountains of north-west Vietnam, about 320 km from Hanoi, near the Chinese border.

2-3 days · Best: September to November and March to May

Phong Nha

Phong Nha is a small, sleepy town on the Son River in central Vietnam, and the gateway to Phong Nha-Ke Bang National Park — a UNESCO World Heritage site and one of the great cave landscapes on Earth.

2-3 days · Best: February to August

Hue

Hue is Vietnam's old imperial capital — slower, quieter and more graceful than Hanoi or Ho Chi Minh City.

2-3 days · Best: February to August
